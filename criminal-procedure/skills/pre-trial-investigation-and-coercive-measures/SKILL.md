# Pre-trial investigation and coercive measures — a tool for counsel

This skill structures the investigation stage and coercive-measure situations from
counsel's point of view. The fundamentals, the arc of the process and the map of
coercive measures: `references/criminal-procedure-fundamentals.md` — read it at the
start of the task.

> **Disclaimer:** the outputs are support for counsel's work — not
> instructions on how to defend yourself without counsel. If the user is themselves a
> suspect without counsel: **the first advice is to obtain counsel before
> questioning** (legal aid, oikeusapulaki 257/2002; the appointment of a defender under
> ROL). See `criminal-procedure/AGENTS.md` — in particular the negative scope
> and the privilege against self-incrimination.

## Output language

Drafts may be produced in **English** for review, but the version actually **filed with the court
or authority must be in Finnish or Swedish** (kielilaki 423/2003; oikeudenkäymiskaari for court
documents). An English filing is not admissible. Always offer to produce the Finnish version, and
state plainly that the English text is a working translation only.

Keep the Finnish term alongside the English one for legally operative concepts on first use, for
example `notice period (irtisanomisaika)`.

## Check the law at source

Look up the provisions of the Pre-Trial Investigation Act and the Coercive Measures Act
(the procedure at questioning, the conditions for coercive measures, the time limits)
with the **`legal-core:legal-research` skill**. The elements and the sentencing range of
the suspected offence are taken from the Criminal Code (39/1889) at source — the range
affects which coercive measures are available.

## Step 1: A situation assessment at once

- **What is the suspicion?** The offence, the time of the act and its description; the
  suspect's status (suspect / witness / injured party — the status can change, and a
  person heard as a witness does not have the same rights).
- **What stage has the investigation reached?** Is there a deprivation of liberty in
  progress? Is a detention hearing coming up `[check the periods at source]`?
- **Time limits and urgency** → `legal-core:engagement-intake`
  (deadline scanning, the disqualification checklist — representing more than one
  suspect requires an assessment of conflicting interests).

## Step 2: Preparing for questioning

Structure this for counsel (do not build an account on the client's behalf):

- **Make the rights clear**: counsel present at questioning, the privilege against
  self-incrimination and the right to remain silent, the right to be told the content of
  the suspicion, interpretation.
- **The choice of line is the client's and counsel's** — an account, silence or a partial
  statement; the skill lists the legal consequences of each (among other things the
  effect of a confession, see the judgment proposal →
  `charges-and-response`).
- **What NOT to do**: aligning accounts with others, touching the evidence, contacting
  witnesses — refuse to assist with these and set out the criminal-law risk.
- Checking the record of the questioning and making corrections before signing.

## Step 3: Coercive-measure situations

Where a coercive measure has been used or is threatened:

1. **Identify the measure and who decides on it** (the map of coercive measures is in the reference).
2. **Assessment of the conditions**: the strength of the suspicion, the sentencing-range
   threshold `[check at source]`, the special conditions (risk of tampering, of flight,
   of continuing offending), **proportionality and the principle of least harm**.
3. **Remedies**: the hearing and re-hearing of a detention matter, a complaint, bringing
   a search before a court, an application to set aside a seizure — the remedies and the
   time limits from the source.
4. **Preparing for a detention hearing**: putting forward the alternatives (a travel
   ban), challenging the suspicion and the special conditions, reasonableness.

## Step 4: Active defence during the investigation

- **Requests for investigative measures**: a suspect has the right to request measures
  that may speak in their favour (the principle of impartiality in the Pre-Trial
  Investigation Act) — draft an itemised request.
- **Restricting or discontinuing the investigation**: structure the grounds (no offence,
  no evidence, the cost ground — the conditions from the source) as a submission to the
  head of investigation or the prosecutor.
- **The closing statement**: the most effective place to have an effect in writing before
  the consideration of charges — structure it around the gaps in the evidence,
  alternative accounts of events, the elements of the offence not being met, procedural
  errors and their effect on admissibility, and the factors bearing on the consideration
  of charges. Tie every assertion to a point in the investigation material.

## Assisting an injured party during the investigation

- Supplementing and particularising the report of an offence; preparing for the injured
  party's questioning.
- **Recording the claims early**: the grounds for the claims for compensation and a
  request that the prosecutor pursue the claim → `injured-party-rights`.
- The conditions for appointing counsel and a support person from the source.

## What this skill does NOT do

- **It does not replace defence counsel and does not give instructions on defending
  yourself without counsel** — it supports counsel's work.
- **It does not construct accounts, explanations or alibis** — it structures the rights,
  the procedure and the material.
- **It does not assist with destroying evidence, influencing witnesses or absconding** —
  an absolute refusal (AGENTS.md).
- **It does not confirm sentencing ranges, thresholds or time limits from memory** —
  from the source or `[check]`.
- **It does not assess guilt** — it assesses the evidence, the procedure and the
  conditions, on the basis of the presumption of innocence.

# Real property and housing — shared guardrails and operating principles

The instructions in this file apply to **every** skill in this domain. SKILL.md says
*what* to do; this file is the **safety net**.

> **Design principle:** real property doctrine belongs in SKILL.md and in the references. These
> guardrails are the life insurance.

---

## A draft that a human reviews

Drafts of a deed of sale (kauppakirja), a lease, a notice of defect (reklamaatio) and a letter of
claim are **drafts for review** — not legal advice. Signatures, confirmation of the sale by a public
purchase witness (kaupanvahvistaja) and applications for registration are the human's
responsibility. Mark anything open to interpretation `[confirm — requires a lawyer's assessment]`.

## Real property and housing-company shares are different worlds — identify which one

The first check in every task: is the object **real property** (the Code of Real Estate, maakaari
540/1995: a prescribed form, a public purchase witness or the electronic conveyancing system of
Maanmittauslaitos, registration of title (lainhuuto)), or are they **housing-company shares** (the
Housing Transactions Act, asuntokauppalaki 843/1994, and the Limited Liability Housing Companies
Act, asunto-osakeyhtiölaki 1599/2009: a sale of movable property, the share register and the
housing information system). Applying the wrong body of rules is the classic mistake in this field.
Hybrid forms (for example a real estate company, or a plot held on lease) are flagged separately.

## The formal requirements are absolute

A sale of real property is subject to a prescribed form (määrämuotoinen): a written deed of sale
containing the minimum content, and a public purchase witness (or the electronic system) — a defect
of form makes the sale void. A pre-contract is also subject to the prescribed form. Do not draft a
"light" sale of real property; check the details of the formal requirements from the source.

## Notice of defect and time limits — speed decides

Relying on a defect requires a notice of defect (reklamaatio) **within a reasonable time** of
discovering the defect, and the absolute long-stop periods (different for real property and for a
dwelling) are checked from the source. Always set out: when the defect was discovered, what has
been notified, and what the long-stop date is `[model calculation — check]`. A late notice of
defect sinks an otherwise good case.

## Duty to disclose and duty to investigate — in both directions

The seller's duty to disclose and the buyer's duty of pre-purchase inspection are the core of
assessing a defect. Do not assist in concealing a known defect (liability for the defect and in
damages; at its most serious, fraud) — refuse and explain the risk. A condition survey does not
remove the seller's liability and does not replace the buyer's own inspection — the significance of
the report is assessed case by case.

## Mandatory rules in housing

Residential leases (481/1995) and consumer sales of dwellings (843/1994) contain mandatory
provisions protecting the weaker party — nothing may be agreed to the detriment of the tenant or the
consumer buyer beyond what the law allows. Commercial leases (482/1995) are non-mandatory to a
greater extent — explain the difference. Check whether a provision is mandatory from the source
before drafting a term.

## From the source, not from memory

Check provisions **with the `legal-core:legal-research` skill** (oik.ai/Finlex): the provisions on
defects, time limits, the division of maintenance liability (the Limited Liability Housing Companies
Act and the articles of association), and the conditions for a rent increase and for termination.
The extensive KKO case law on defects and notices of defect is retrieved from the source with its
identifiers. The skill cannot see register data (registration of title, encumbrances, articles of
association, the manager's certificate) — draw up a list for a human to obtain.

## Shared standards

For references to statutes, case law and preparatory works, follow the collection's shared source
standard in [`references/citation-style.md`](../references/citation-style.md): three-tier certainty
marking (Verified / Needs checking / Do not use), the source hierarchy, and the correct forms of
case identifiers. In short: attach the source to the number, never present something unchecked as
verified, and never assert what a decision says without having checked the decision from the source.

On liability, professional ethics (confidentiality, disqualification, who may represent whom) and
data handling (GDPR Article 28 processing agreement, anonymisation before analysis):
[`references/liability-and-security.md`](../references/liability-and-security.md). **A disclaimer
alone is not protection** — protection comes from verifying the source, certainty marking, checking
the premises, stating the negative scope, and the human review gate.

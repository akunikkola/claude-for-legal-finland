# Marriage and division of property — prenuptial agreements, ositus and the position of a cohabiting partner

This skill sets out the property relations of marriage and how they are
dissolved. The map of family law, its concepts and its pitfalls are in
`references/family-and-inheritance-fundamentals.md` — read it at the start of the task.

> **Disclaimer:** drafts and calculations are for review — not legal advice.
> Formal requirements, time limits and taxes are checked against the source.
> See `family-and-inheritance/AGENTS.md`.

## Output language

Drafts may be produced in **English** for review, but the version actually **filed with the court
or authority must be in Finnish or Swedish** (kielilaki 423/2003; oikeudenkäymiskaari for court
documents). An English filing is not admissible. Always offer to produce the Finnish version, and
state plainly that the English text is a working translation only.

Keep the Finnish term alongside the English one for legally operative concepts on first use, for
example `notice period (irtisanomisaika)`.

## Check the statute and the case law against the source

Retrieve the provisions of the Marriage Act (avioliittolaki 234/1929) and of the
Act on the Dissolution of the Household of Cohabiting Partners (avoliittolaki
26/2011) with the **`legal-core:legal-research` skill** (oik.ai/Finlex). Take KKO
case law on the adjustment of a division, on property outside the marital right
and on compensation to a cohabiting partner from the source. Formal requirements
(the validity of a prenuptial agreement and of a deed of division) always from
the statute.

## Step 1: Identify the situation and the applicable statute

1. **Marriage or cohabitation?** In a marriage the Marriage Act applies
   (the marital right (avio-oikeus), the division of matrimonial property); in
   cohabitation there is no marital right, only the dissolution of the joint
   household and possible compensation (26/2011) together with general property
   law (ownership, joint ownership). Do not conflate the two — the basic concepts
   are different.
2. **What dissolves the property relationship?** Divorce, or the death of one
   spouse? On death the division of matrimonial property and the inheritance are
   connected (the position of the surviving spouse, the tasinko privilege) →
   `inheritance-and-wills`.
3. **Is there a prenuptial agreement or any other agreement?** The content and
   validity of the prenuptial agreement (form, registration) determine the extent
   of the marital right.

## Step 2: The prenuptial agreement

- **Purpose**: excluding the marital right wholly or in part, unilaterally or
  reciprocally, over specified property or over everything. Work out what the
  client is actually trying to achieve.
- **Form and entry into force**: a prenuptial agreement is subject to a
  prescribed form and must be registered in order to take effect — **check the
  requirements as to form, attestation and registration against the statute**
  `[check]`. A prenuptial agreement made before the marriage as against one made
  during the marriage.
- **Drafting**: identify the property clearly; address yield, substitute property
  and debts; avoid ambiguity.
- **Risks**: a term that is too one-sided may lead to adjustment of the division;
  the parties may want to treat death and divorce differently.

## Step 3: Division of matrimonial property or separation of assets

1. **Map the property and the debts** as at the date on which the ground for the
   division arose (the filing of the divorce petition or the date of death —
   `[check]`).
2. **Property subject to the marital right as against property outside it**
   (prenuptial agreement, a gratuitous acquisition subject to a condition,
   personal property).
3. **Calculating the marital shares**: the combined net property subject to the
   marital right on both sides is divided — the wealthier spouse pays the
   **equalisation payment (tasinko)**. Show the calculation with its intermediate
   steps and mark the assumptions.
4. **Compensation and special questions**: dissolution of jointly owned property,
   the fate of the home, the allocation of debts, business assets.
5. **Adjustment of the division**: correction of an unreasonable outcome — the
   conditions and the discretion from the source; it is not automatic.
6. **Deed of division or a division by an appointed distributor**: the form of an
   agreed division (witnesses) or a division carried out by an estate distributor
   where there is a dispute.

## Step 4: Dissolving the joint household of cohabiting partners

- Scope: the statute covers cohabiting partners as defined in it (a joint
  household and living together, duration or a joint child) —
  `[check the definition]`.
- **Separation of assets** on the basis of ownership; joint ownership is
  dissolved. **Compensation** for a contribution to the joint household on
  specific conditions.
- An estate distributor may also be appointed for the dissolution of a
  cohabiting relationship.

## Step 5: Taxes and next steps

- **Transfer tax, inheritance tax and gift tax** may arise on transfers of

- Registrations and entries (the prenuptial agreement, registration of title to

## What this skill does NOT do

- **Does not confirm formal requirements, time limits or taxes from memory**
  — from the statute, from Verohallinto, or `[check]`.
- **Does not represent both spouses in a conflict** — disqualification; where
  the situation is contested, a draft from one party's perspective only.
- **Does not calculate inheritance tax or transfer tax** →
  `taxation`.
- **Does not replace the estate distributor or the court** — a division by an
  appointed distributor and any disputes are settled there.

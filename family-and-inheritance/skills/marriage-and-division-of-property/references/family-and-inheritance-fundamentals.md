## Key distinctions

- **Marriage as against cohabitation.** In a marriage there is a marital right
  (avio-oikeus) to the other's property (the division of matrimonial property, the
  equalisation payment). In cohabitation there is no marital right; on dissolution the
  assets are separated according to ownership and there may be compensation (26/2011).
  A different set of concepts.
- **The marital right as against ownership.** The marital right does not make a spouse
  an owner during the marriage; it takes effect only in the division. Ownership and the
  marital right are two different things.
- **Property subject to the marital right as against property outside it.** A
  prenuptial agreement or a condition attached to an acquisition can place property
  outside the division.
- **The division of matrimonial property as against the distribution of an estate.** On
  death the division of matrimonial property is carried out first (between the spouses)
  and the distribution of the inheritance after it (among the heirs). Keep the stages
  apart.
- **Statutory succession as against a will.** The Code of Inheritance fixes the order of
  succession (the parentela classes); a will can change it within the limits of the
  compulsory share.
- **The compulsory share is mandatory.** A direct heir is entitled to the compulsory
  share (lakiosa) regardless of a will; it must be claimed within a time limit
  (`[check]`).

## The best interests of the child, and safety

In a decision concerning a child the criterion is **the best interests of the child**,
not the wishes of the parents as such. Domestic violence, the threat of it or any other
safety concern must always be identified, and it comes before contact and custody
arrangements. In an acute situation direct the user to the police, to child welfare
services or to a shelter.

## Form and time limits — absolute, and to be checked

Many documents in family and inheritance law are subject to a **prescribed form** (a
will, a prenuptial agreement, certain agreements): a defect in form or in attestation
can render the document void. Time limits are strict too (among others the estate
inventory, claiming the compulsory share, contesting a will, contesting a division of
matrimonial property or a distribution of an estate). **Do not state a formal
requirement or a time limit from memory** — check it against the statute and mark the
assumptions.

## Typical pitfalls

- Conflating the concepts of marriage and cohabitation.
- Bundling together the stages of the division of matrimonial property and the
  distribution of an estate on death.
- Overlooking the position of the surviving spouse (among other things the right of
  possession of the joint home and the tasinko privilege).
- Forgetting the compulsory share when drafting a will.
- Assessing tax consequences (inheritance tax, gift tax, transfer tax) from memory.
- Disqualification: assisting both parties to the same dispute.

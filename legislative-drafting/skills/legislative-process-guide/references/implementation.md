# Implementation and monitoring

Once an act has entered into force, responsibility moves to implementation: the
authorities are given guidance and training, and the effects of the act are
monitored. Good implementation is planned already during the preparation — the
implementation and monitoring plan is drawn up in the basic preparation and
updated as the process goes on.

## Planning the implementation

The implementation and monitoring plan deals with:

- the measures relating to entry into force and the timetable,
- the authorities responsible for implementation and the division of responsibilities,
- the necessary decrees, administrative regulations and instructions,
- the appropriations needed and their inclusion in the budget,
- the training needs of the staff,
- changes to information systems, and
- communication to the different target groups.

**Outcomes:** the implementation and monitoring plan; the implementation
instructions.

## Entry into force

- An act enters into force at the time laid down in it. Entry into force may also
  be laid down separately (for example by a Government decree) where the act so
  provides.
- Entry into force may involve **transitional provisions** defining how the new
  act applies to situations that arose before it entered into force.
- Entry into force is communicated to the authorities, to the stakeholders and,
  where necessary, to the general public; regulation of EU origin is notified to
  the Commission (notification).

## Guidance and training

Those responsible for implementation are given instructions on application
(principles, guidance on interpretation, examples, the division of
responsibilities) and training. The target groups are the administrative
authorities, where necessary the courts and other bodies applying the act, and
the end users.

**Outcomes:** instructions on application; training and guidance materials.

## Monitoring and ex-post evaluation

After the act enters into force, the following are monitored:

1. **Whether the objectives are being met** — did the act achieve the objectives set for it?
2. **The impacts** — economic, administrative, environmental and other impacts on
   society, compared with what was estimated in the government bill.
3. **Compliance and workability** — problems in application, differences in
   interpretation, the findings of supervision.
4. **Feedback** — from the authorities, the stakeholders and the public.

The results of the monitoring are documented (monitoring reports, ex-post
evaluations) and form the evidence base for any project to amend the act.

**Outcomes:** a monitoring report; an ex-post evaluation; recommendations on the
need for change.

## Amending or repealing an act

If the monitoring reveals shortcomings or circumstances change, the act may be
amended or repealed. A project to amend or repeal goes through the same
legislative process: initiative → preliminary preparation → basic preparation →
consultation procedure → further preparation → Government decision-making →
consideration in Eduskunta (two readings) → confirmation by the President of the
Republic → publication in the statute book. See
`decision-making-and-parliament.md`.

## Publication and keeping up to date

- A confirmed act is published in the **Statute Book of Finland** and is
  available in **Finlex** (https://www.finlex.fi/), which also maintains an
  up-to-date consolidated version.
- The records of the consideration in Eduskunta (reports, statements, minutes)
  are on the Eduskunta website; the preparatory material of a project is in
  Hankeikkuna.

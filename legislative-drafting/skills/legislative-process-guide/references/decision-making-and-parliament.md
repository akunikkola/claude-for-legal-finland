# Government decision-making and consideration in Eduskunta (stages 5–7)

After the further preparation the government bill is considered by the
Government and submitted to Eduskunta. Eduskunta considers the legislative
proposal, and the act passed is confirmed by the President of the Republic.

## 5. Government decision-making

**Who:** the presenting official, the minister and the plenary session of the Government.

- Matters of economic significance are first considered in the **Finance
  Committee of the Government** (raha-asiainvaliokunta).
- **The plenary session of the Government decides on submitting the government
  bill to Eduskunta.** The matter is presented by the presenting official of the
  ministry; the Chancellor of Justice oversees the legality of the proceedings
  in the Government.
- The submission of the bill is communicated (a press release, Hankeikkuna, the
  website).

**Outcomes:** the government bill submitted to Eduskunta; a press release.

**Statutes:** Suomen perustuslaki (731/1999); valtioneuvoston ohjesääntö
(262/2003).

## 6. Consideration in Eduskunta

The consideration of a legislative proposal in Eduskunta is laid down in
perustuslaki 72 § and in the Rules of Procedure of Eduskunta (eduskunnan
työjärjestys 40/2000). The course of it:

1. **The preliminary debate** (lähetekeskustelu) in plenary session — the bill is
   debated and the matter is sent to a committee.
2. **The committee stage** — the committee responsible for the matter draws up a
   **report** (mietintö); other committees may give statements. The committee
   hears experts, and the Constitutional Law Committee assesses the legislative
   procedure where necessary.
3. **The first reading** in plenary session — on the basis of the report a
   general debate and a detailed consideration are held; **the content of the
   legislative proposal is decided**.
4. **The second reading** — at the earliest on the third day after the end of the
   first reading; **the approval or rejection of the legislative proposal is
   decided**.
5. **The reply of Eduskunta** is transmitted to the Government.

A legislative proposal is therefore considered in **two plenary readings** — not
in "three readings".

**A special case:** a proposal to enact or amend the Constitution is considered
in the procedure laid down in perustuslaki 73 § (left in abeyance over an
election and passed by a two-thirds majority, or declared urgent by a
five-sixths majority).

**Outcomes:** the act passed by Eduskunta (the reply of Eduskunta); the reports
and statements of the committees; the minutes of the plenary session.

## 7. Confirmation, publication and entry into force of the act

1. **The President of the Republic confirms the act** within three months of its
   being submitted for confirmation (perustuslaki 77 §).
2. If the President **does not confirm** the act, it returns to Eduskunta for
   consideration; if Eduskunta passes the act again without changing its
   substance, it enters into force without confirmation (perustuslaki 78 §).
3. **Publication:** the act is published in the Statute Book of Finland
   (perustuslaki 79 §; Act on the Statute Book of Finland, laki Suomen
   säädöskokoelmasta 188/2000). The act must show when it enters into force.
4. **Entry into force:** the act enters into force at the time laid down in it;
   where no time has been laid down, entry into force is laid down separately.

**Outcomes:** the act confirmed, published in the statute book and entering into
force; a press release; the move to implementation (see
`implementation.md`).

## Duration

The duration of the consideration in Eduskunta varies from weeks to more than a
year, depending on the scope of the matter and how contested it is. Urgent
consideration is possible, but the stages are the same. Do not promise a project
a timetable on behalf of Eduskunta — mark timetable estimates as estimates.

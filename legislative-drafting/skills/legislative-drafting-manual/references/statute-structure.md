# The structure of a statute

The structure of a statute means how the statute is divided into parts. That structure is highly regular and consistent, which helps in reading and understanding statutes.

> The model statutory text in the code blocks below stays in Finnish. It is text a drafter copies into a Finnish statute, not an illustration to be translated.

## The order of the parts of a statute

A statute is built in the following order:

1. **Johtolause** — the enacting formula, the introductory text of the statute
2. **Alkusäännökset** — the opening provisions, introductory and defining provisions
3. **Asiasäännökset** — the substantive provisions, the actual content of the statute
4. **Siirtymäsäännökset** — the transitional provisions on moving from the old statute to the new one
5. **Voimaantulolauseke** — the entry-into-force clause, stating when the statute enters into force
6. **Kumoamislauseke** — the repeal clause, the provision on repealing statutes

## The main parts of a statute

### Johtolause — the enacting formula

The enacting formula is the text at the beginning of a statute, containing:

- The information on who issues the statute
- References to the legal bases for issuing it (for example in the Constitution or in a higher-level statute)
- Any information on the entry into force of the statute
- Any short description of the content of the statute

The enacting formula is written in a particularly formal way and follows certain established expressions.

### Alkusäännökset — the opening provisions

The opening provisions comprise:

- **Soveltamisala (scope of application)** — defining the scope of the statute (to whom, to what and to which situations it applies)
- **Määritelmät (definitions)** — definitions of the terms used in the statute
- **Other introductory provisions** — the other provisions needed to understand the statute

The opening provisions must be placed at the beginning of the statute, before the substantive provisions.

### Asiasäännökset — the substantive provisions

The substantive provisions are the actual content of the statute. They are divided into:

- **Luvut (chapters)** — into which the largest statutes are divided
- **Pykälät (sections)** — the section is the basic numbered unit of a statute (1 §, 2 § and so on)
- **Momentit (subsections)** — sections may be divided into subsections

#### The structure of a section

A section may contain several subsections. The first subsection is the so-called main subsection, and where a section has several parts the others are numbered 2 momentti, 3 momentti and so on.

Sections are numbered consecutively throughout the statute.

#### An example of subsection numbering

```
1 §

Tämä momentti on ensimmäinen momentti eli päämomenttti.

2 momentti. Tämä on pykälän toinen momentti.

3 momentti. Tämä on pykälän kolmas momentti.
```

Or, where a section has only one subsection, no numbering is needed:

```
1 §

Tämä pykälä sisältää vain yhden säännöksen.
```

### Using chapters

Chapter numbering is optional, but in large statutes (more than 100 sections) it is advisable to use chapters for clarity. Chapters are numbered and have headings.

### Siirtymäsäännökset — the transitional provisions

The transitional provisions define how the move between the old and the new statute is made. These provisions matter when:

- The old statute is repealed
- Rights and obligations are transferred
- Matters already in process pass over to the new provisions

### Voimaantulolauseke — the entry-into-force clause

The entry-into-force clause lays down when the statute enters into force. Entry into force may be:

- The date on which the statute is confirmed
- A named date in the future
- A relative time (for example one month after confirmation)

Example: "Tämä laki tulee voimaan 1 päivänä tammikuuta 2024."

### Kumoamislauseke — the repeal clause

The repeal clause lays down which previously applicable statutes are repealed in whole or in part.

Example: "Kumotaan laki X päivän y kuusta z vuonna n annetusta laista."

## Numbering in statutes

### Sections

Sections are numbered as a single consecutive series throughout the statute. The sections of a statute are numbered from 1 § onwards.

The number of a section is marked with the § symbol:
- 1 §, 2 § — read as ordinals: the first section, the second section

### Subsections

Where a section has several parts (subsections), the number of the first subsection is left out. The other subsections are numbered 2 momentti, 3 momentti and so on.

Example:
```
5 §

Ensimmäinen momentti (numerointi jätetään pois).

2 momentti. Toinen momentti.

3 momentti. Kolmas momentti.
```

### Chapters

Where a statute is divided into chapters, the chapters are numbered with Roman numerals (I, II, III and so on) or using Arabic numerals (1, 2, 3 and so on). The sections under each chapter are numbered consecutively.

## The principles of arranging a statute

### Clarity and consistency

The structure of a statute must be clear and consistent. This means:

- Matters that belong together are kept close to one another
- Similar provisions are grouped together
- Logical wholes form coherent parts

### Uniformity of form

Provisions at the same level are written in the same way. For example, if a verb phrase is used in one section, the same structure is used in the other sections too.

### The order of provisions

Provisions are arranged logically:

- General provisions precede special provisions
- Provisions on granting a permission come before those on obligations
- Procedural provisions come after the substantive provisions

## Particular structures

### Lists and enumerations

When a statute enumerates several matters, the following structure is often used:

```
1 §

Säädöksessä tarkoitetaan:
1) ensimmäinen asia
2) toinen asia
3) kolmas asia
```

### Provisions creating exceptions

Provisions creating exceptions are often written as separate sections or subsections setting out the exception to the main rule.

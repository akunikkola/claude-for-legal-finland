# Types of statute and the level of norm

Statutes form a hierarchy and each type has its own place in it. Choosing the
type (an act or a decree, a new statute or an amending statute) is one of the
fundamental decisions in legislative drafting.

## The hierarchy of statutes

1. **Perustuslaki** (731/1999) — the foundations of the system of government
   and the fundamental rights. A statute below the level of an act may not be
   applied if it conflicts with the Constitution or with another act
   (perustuslaki 107 §).
2. **Laki (an act)** — a statute enacted by Eduskunta.
3. **Asetus (a decree)** — a statute issued by the President of the Republic,
   the Government or a ministry on the basis of an authorisation laid down in
   the Constitution or in another act (perustuslaki 80 §).
4. **Legal rules and regulations of an authority** — another authority may be
   authorised by an act to issue legal rules on specified matters, where there
   are particular reasons connected with the subject of the regulation
   (perustuslaki 80 § 2 mom.).

In addition, EU law applies (regulations as directly applicable, directives as
nationally implemented) together with the international obligations binding on
Finland.

## Laki — an act

- Legislative power is exercised by Eduskunta. A legislative proposal is
  initiated by a government bill or by a private member's bill (perustuslaki
  70 §).
- Eduskunta considers a legislative proposal in **two plenary readings** after
  the committee stage (perustuslaki 72 §) and passes an ordinary act by a
  **simple majority**.
- **The procedure for enacting constitutional law** (perustuslaki 73 §): the
  proposal is first left in abeyance over an election and after the election is
  passed by a **two-thirds majority** — or it is declared urgent by a
  **five-sixths majority** and then passed by a two-thirds majority. The
  five-sixths requirement therefore concerns the declaration of urgency, not
  the passing of the proposal.
- **Matters to be laid down by an act:** the principles governing the rights
  and obligations of the individual, and matters that under the Constitution
  otherwise fall within the province of an act, must be laid down by an act
  (perustuslaki 80 § 1 mom.).

## Asetus — a decree

Issuing a decree requires an **authorising provision** in the Constitution or
in another act (perustuslaki 80 §). There are three issuers of decrees:

- **A Government decree** — the main rule: where the issuer of a decree is not
  separately laid down, the decree is issued by the Government.
- **A ministerial decree** — matters that are technical in nature and of lesser
  social significance.
- **A decree of the President of the Republic** — today a narrow field of
  application, connected among other things with bringing international
  obligations into force and with treaty decrees concerning Åland.

A decree may not conflict with an act, and a decree cannot lay down provisions
on matters falling within the province of an act. The enacting formula of a
decree states the authorising provision (see `enacting-formula.md`).

## A new statute, an amending statute and repeal

### A new statute

Issued when the matter to be regulated is new, or when so many changes would
have to be made to the statute in force that a full reform is clearer. A new
act usually repeals the old one at the same time (a repeal provision in the
final provisions).

### An amending statute

Amends a statute in force: provisions are amended, added or repealed. The
enacting formula of an amending statute identifies the parts being changed
(muutetaan / lisätään / kumotaan — amended / added / repealed). In amending, the
structure and numbering of the original statute are followed; new sections are
added where necessary as a-sections (for example 5 a §).

### A repealing statute

Repeals a statute or part of it without replacing regulation. Where a statute
is repealed, **transitional provisions** are laid down as necessary on how
situations arising under the old statute are treated.

## The basic rules on applying statutes

- **Hierarchy (lex superior):** the lower-level norm gives way where it
  conflicts with a higher-level one; the primacy of the Constitution is laid
  down in perustuslaki 106–107 §.
- **Later in time (lex posterior):** as between norms of the same level, the
  later displaces the earlier.
- **Speciality (lex specialis):** a special provision displaces a general one.

## The passing of an act in brief

1. The plenary session of the Government submits the government bill to
   Eduskunta.
2. Eduskunta considers the legislative proposal in two plenary readings after
   the committee stage.
3. The President of the Republic confirms the act (perustuslaki 77 §).
4. The act is published in the Statute Book of Finland and enters into force at
   the time laid down in it (perustuslaki 79 §).

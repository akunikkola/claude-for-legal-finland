# Lakikieli — symbols and inflected forms

This guide is based on Lainkirjoittajan opas, chapter 24.4 "Merkeistä ja taivutusmuodoista lakikielessä". It sets out the main rules for notation in Finnish statutory language.

> The rules below govern **Finnish** statutory text. The examples, inflected forms and word lists are given in Finnish because they *are* the rule — do not translate them.

## Using the section symbol

### The basic principle

The section symbol (§) is **always** used with the number of the section, and a section number is **always** used with the section symbol.

**Example:** *2 §:ssä tarkoitettu ilmoitus*

### Reference without an ordinal number

When referring to a section without an ordinal number, the word "pykälä" is written out.

**Examples:**
- *tässä pykälässä*
- *kolmessa ensin mainitussa pykälässä*

### Inflected forms

In inflected forms the case endings are written after the section symbol.

**Examples:**
- 2 §:n
- 3–5 §:ssä

### Referring to several sections

The word "pykälä" is in the **singular** even when referring to several sections.

**Correct:**
- 2 ja 4 §:ssä
- 3–5 §:ään

**Incorrect:**
- ~~2 ja 4 §:issä~~
- ~~2 ja 4 §§:ssä~~
- ~~3–5 §:iin~~

### Interpreting a range of sections (2–4 §)

If a statute refers to 2–4 §, it depends on the context whether this is read as a reference to "2, 3 ja 4 §:ään" or to "2, 3 tai 4 §:ään".

**Examples:**
- *hakija, jonka omistama yritys täyttää 2–4 §:ssä säädetyt edellytykset* → read as: "2, 3 ja 4 §:ssä"
- *jos jokin 2–4 §:ssä säädetyistä edellytyksistä täyttyy* → read as: "2, 3 tai 4 §:ssä"

If the context does not settle the interpretation, the list is written out in full: "2, 3 ja 4 §" or "2, 3 tai 4 §".

### A-sections in lists

If a list of sections includes a-sections (for example 2 a §, 3 b §), they cannot be replaced by a dash but are mentioned separately.

**Example:** 4 a—4 d § (not "4 a, 4 b, 4 c ja 4 d §")

If the a-sections are not included in the list, this has to be expressed explicitly: 2–4 ja 5 §, where the statute has a 4 a § that is not included in the list.

## Ordinal numbers and dates

### Ordinal numbers

**No full stop is used** after the ordinal number of a section or another provision.

**Correct:**
- 2 luku
- 2 §
- 2 momentti
- 2 kohta
- 2 luvun 2 §:n 2 momentin 2 kohta

### Dates in statutory text

Dates are written without an ordinal number.

**Example:** *18 päivänä lokakuuta 2011*

### Dates in the reasoning of a government bill

In the reasoning text of a government bill dates are written in figures.

**Example:** 1.6.2020

## Numerals

### The basic principle

- The numbers 1–10 are usually written **in words** (yksi, kaksi, kolme and so on)
- The numbers 11 and above are written **in figures**

### Exceptions

- With units of measurement and in tables, figures are used for 1–10 as well
- If numbers from 1–10 and larger numbers occur in the same context, the numbers 1–10 are also written in figures

### Large numbers

Large numbers are divided into groups of three digits separated by a space.

**Correct:**
- 1 000
- 2 250

**Incorrect:**
- ~~1000~~
- ~~1.000~~

### Numbers repeated in parentheses

In statutory language a number written in words is **not** repeated in figures in parentheses.

## Percentages

- The percent sign (%) is used in statutory language **only in tables**
- The % sign may be used in the reasoning of a government bill, but the notation must be consistent (either "20 %" or "20 prosenttia" throughout)
- In statutory text (other than tables) the form **"prosenttia"** is used

## Units of measurement

- Units of measurement are usually written **unabbreviated** in statutory language
- Abbreviations of units are used only in tables and in statutes of a technical nature
- The symbols laid down in the Government decree on units of measurement (1015/2014) are used as the abbreviations

## Monetary units

Monetary units are written **in words**.

**Examples:**
- 50 euroa
- 200 kruunua

## Beginning a sentence

In statutory language **a sentence never begins with a numeral**.

**Correct:** *Edellä 2 §:ssä...*

**Note:** within a sentence the words "edellä" and "jäljempänä" are not used, as they are unnecessary.

**Exception:** the text of a paragraph item may begin directly with a numeral, without adding "edellä" before it.

## Punctuation in introductory paragraphs and paragraph items

### The introductory paragraph

- The introductory paragraph of a section or subsection ends with a **colon**
- The items that follow it begin with a **lower-case initial letter**
- Items are separated from one another by a **semicolon**
- Between the last two items a conjunction is used where needed: *ja*, *tai*, *sekä* or *taikka*

### Sub-items

- The introductory paragraph of an item ends with a colon
- Sub-items are separated from one another by a semicolon
- Between the last two sub-items a conjunction is used where needed

## Using abbreviations

### The main rule

**Abbreviations are not used** in statutory text (apart from the exceptions below).

### Exceptional letter abbreviations

Letter abbreviations may exceptionally be used in statutory text, for example for the name of an organisation.

**Important:**
- Even well-known abbreviations (EU, YK, OECD) must be **explained** on first use
- The abbreviation is placed in parentheses after the name of the organisation
- The abbreviation is not italicised

**Example:** *Euroopan unioni (EU)*

### Titles of statutes

Letter abbreviations of the titles of statutes are **not used** in statutory text.

### The reasoning of a government bill

Abbreviations (for example OK, RL) may be used in the reasoning of a government bill if, on first mention, they are placed in parentheses after the statute number and separated from it by a semicolon.

**Example:** *oikeudenkäynnistä hallintoasioissa annettu laki (808/2019; HOL)*

**Not permitted:** hash-style abbreviations (risuaitalyhenteet), for example SopMenL, are not allowed even in the reasoning text.

### Established abbreviations in the reasoning

The following established abbreviations are used in the reasoning of government bills without being explained:
- EN
- ETA
- EU
- ETYJ
- OECD
- WTO
- YK

## What this skill does NOT do

- **Does not replace official statutory preparation or the ministry's responsibility.** Checking notation supports the preparation, and the official responsible for the preparation is answerable for the result.
- **Does not confirm statute numbers, section numbers or the wording of a cited statute from memory**; correctness is checked in Finlex even where the notation is formally right.
- **Does not cover the content or structure of a statute or the formulation of substantive provisions** — only the symbols, numbers and inflected forms under chapter 24.4.
- **Does not settle standard-language spelling, comma use or compound words** any further than the special rules of statutory language require.
- **Does not guarantee that the interpretation of a range of sections (for example 2–4 §) is correct**; the interpretation of the context is settled by the drafter.

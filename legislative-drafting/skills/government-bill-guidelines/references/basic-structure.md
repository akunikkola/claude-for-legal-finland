# The basic structure of a government bill

## Overview

A government bill with the basic structure is the simplest structure, used in most cases. It consists of the reasoning (perustelut), the ponsi and the draft acts.

> The structural outline below is the **Finnish template** a drafter copies into the bill. The part titles stay in Finnish; the English gloss follows each of them in the guidance.

## The full structure

```
SISÄLLYS
PERUSTELUT
  1 Asian tausta ja valmistelu
    1.1 Tausta
    1.2 Valmistelu
  2 Nykytila ja sen arviointi
  3 Tavoitteet
  4 Ehdotukset ja niiden vaikutukset
    4.1 Keskeiset ehdotukset
    4.2 Pääasialliset vaikutukset
  5 Muut toteuttamisvaihtoehdot
    5.1 Vaihtoehdot
    5.2 Ulkomaiden lainsäädäntö
  6 Lausuntopalaute
  7 Säännöskohtaiset perustelut
  8 Lakia alemman asteinen sääntely
  9 Voimaantulo
  10 Toimeenpano ja seuranta
  11 Suhde muihin esityksiin
    11.1 Esityksen riippuvuus muista esityksistä
    11.2 Suhde talousarvioesitykseen

Rinnakkaisteksti / Rinnakkaistekstit

```

## A detailed guide to the reasoning

### 1 Asian tausta ja valmistelu — background and preparation

This part sets out what matter is being dealt with and how it was arrived at.

#### 1.1 Tausta — background

The background states:
- Why the legislation is being changed
- What problem or shortcoming the change corrects
- The policy statements in the Government Programme bearing on the matter (where available)
- The international commitments the change relates to

**Include the following:**
- The relevance and significance of the matter for society
- Government policy and the objectives as regards the matter
- Earlier preparatory work and its conclusions
- International obligations and agreements (EU, YK, others)

**Tip**: the background part must state why it is necessary to change the act. Do not merely describe the present situation; explain what problems or shortcomings the change resolves.

#### 1.2 Valmistelu — preparation

The preparation part describes:
- The course of the preparation process
- The key stages during the preparation
- The parties who took part (ministries, experts, partners)
- The statements given and the parties who gave them

**Include the following:**
- When the preparation began and how long it took
- Who led the preparation and the ministry responsible
- The ministries and other parties who took part
- The expert groups and advisory boards
- The forms of international co-operation (where relevant)

**Tip**: say what was done during the preparation and who took part. This shows the scope and the breadth of the preparation.

### 2 Nykytila ja sen arviointi — the current state and its assessment

This part describes:
- The content and the application of the legislation in force
- How the statutes work in practice
- The problems and shortcomings that have emerged
- A comparison with the statutes of other countries and with international standards

**Drafting instructions:**
- Describe the legislation in force clearly
- Identify and define the problem areas concretely
- Use examples and statistics
- Compare with international solutions (where relevant)

**Include an analytical assessment:**
- Where the legislation in force does not work well
- What difficulties have emerged in applying it in practice
- What problems the present system causes
- Whether there are problems in the points where a change is proposed

### 3 Tavoitteet — objectives

The objectives part defines what the legislative change is meant to achieve.

**The objectives may be:**
- General objectives (for example improving legal protection)
- Concrete objectives (for example shortening processing times)
- Economic objectives (for example cost savings)
- Objectives concerning society

**Drafting instructions:**
- The objectives must be expressed clearly and measurably
- Every proposal is grounded on particular objectives
- The objectives are addressed primarily to the Government and to Eduskunta
- The objectives may be both qualitative and quantitative

### 4 Ehdotukset ja niiden vaikutukset — the proposals and their impacts

This is the most central part of a government bill. It sets out concretely what changes are proposed and how they take effect.

#### 4.1 Keskeiset ehdotukset — the key proposals

The key proposals describe:
- What new provisions are proposed
- What existing provisions are amended
- What provisions are removed

**Drafting instructions:**
- Present the proposals clearly and consistently
- Group the proposals logically (for example by scope or by subject area)
- Use examples to make them concrete
- Explain why each change is necessary

**Note:** this part does not describe every change to a provision in detail. The detailed reasoning goes in part 7 (Säännöskohtaiset perustelut).

#### 4.2 Pääasialliset vaikutukset — the main impacts

A clear picture must be given of the impacts:
- The effect of the amendment on the different parties (municipalities, businesses, citizens and so on)
- Administrative impacts
- Economic impacts
- Social impacts

**The account of the impacts must cover:**
- The favourable impacts
- The adverse or burdensome impacts
- The impacts on different population groups
- The impacts on sectors
- The international impacts (where relevant)

### 5 Muut toteuttamisvaihtoehdot — other implementation options

This part sets out the other possible solutions that were considered but not proposed.

#### 5.1 Vaihtoehdot — the alternatives

The alternatives that may be presented include:
- Differences in the scope of the regulation
- Different technical solutions for implementation
- Partial changes instead of full ones

**Drafting instructions:**
- Present the idea behind each alternative clearly
- Justify why the alternative was rejected
- Compare the chosen solution with the rejected alternatives
- Write objectively

#### 5.2 Ulkomaiden lainsäädäntö — foreign legislation

This sub-part may describe:
- How the corresponding matter has been resolved in other countries
- International standards and recommendations
- The solutions in use at EU level

**Use the information:**
- To show that the chosen solution is one found good internationally
- Or to justify why we depart from international practice

### 6 Lausuntopalaute — consultation feedback

The consultation feedback part deals with the statements given on the preparation of the government bill and the responses to them.

**Content:**
- A summary of the statements received
- The key observations and proposals
- The Government's responses to the significant observations
- The changes made as a result of the statements

**Drafting instructions:**
- Go through all the statements received
- Group the statements by subject
- Respond with reasons to all the significant observations
- State what changes were made on the basis of the statements

### 7 Säännöskohtaiset perustelut — section-by-section reasoning

This is the detailed part, giving the reasoning for every provision of the draft act.

**Structure:**
- Follows the structure of the draft act
- Deals with it chapter by chapter and section by section
- The reasoning may contain sub-parts

**Drafting instructions:**
- Explain the content of every provision
- Justify why the provision is necessary
- Explain how the provision is to be applied
- Use examples to make it concrete

**Note:** the section-by-section reasoning may be highly detailed and technical. Write clearly nonetheless, so that experts can understand and assess the proposals.

### 8 Lakia alemman asteinen sääntely — regulation below the level of an act

This part deals with decrees and other statutes below the level of an act.

**Content:**
- Which decrees the amendment affects
- What new decrees need to be issued
- Which decrees have to be amended
- The timetables

**Drafting instructions:**
- List the decrees the amendment affects
- Explain the content of each decree briefly
- Justify why the decrees have to be amended

### 9 Voimaantulo — entry into force

The entry-into-force part defines when the act enters into force.

**Content:**
- The date of entry into force (or the provision on the time of entry into force)
- The reasons for the time of entry into force (where we depart from the ordinary practice)
- Transitional provisions (where they are needed)

**Drafting instructions:**
- Be clear about the date of entry into force
- Justify the time of entry into force chosen
- Mention the transitional provisions

**Examples of entry-into-force wording:**
- "Laki tulee voimaan 1.1.2020."
- "Laki tulee voimaan 14 päivää sen julkaisemisen jälkeen."
- "Laki tulee voimaan päivää ennen kuin direktiivi on pantava täytäntöön."

### 10 Toimeenpano ja seuranta — implementation and monitoring

The implementation part deals with how the act is put into effect and how its workability is monitored.

**Content:**
- Which bodies are responsible for implementation
- The timetables for implementation
- The monitoring arrangements
- The reporting obligations

**Drafting instructions:**
- Name the ministry and the authorities responsible
- Describe the monitoring arrangements
- Set the times of evaluation (where they are already known)

### 11 Suhde muihin esityksiin — the relationship to other bills

This part describes how this bill relates to other government bills or legislative projects.

#### 11.1 Esityksen riippuvuus muista esityksistä — the bill's dependence on other bills

- Is the bill dependent on other bills being dealt with at the same time?
- Does the bill require other bills to enter into force?

#### 11.2 Suhde talousarvioesitykseen — the relationship to the budget proposal

- Does the bill affect public expenditure or revenue?
- Have budget authorisations or appropriations been included for it?

### 12 Suhde perustuslakiin ja säätämisjärjestys — the relationship to the Constitution and the legislative procedure

This part deals with the relationship of the government bill to the Constitution and to the legislative procedure.

**Content:**
- How the Constitution applies to the change
- The legislative procedure (ordinary, or one requiring the fuller procedure)
- The special provisions relating to the change

**Drafting instructions:**
- Show that the proposal complies with the Constitution
- Mention any aspects affecting fundamental rights
- State the legislative procedure

## Guidance on the whole

### General principles

- **Clarity**: every part must be written clearly
- **Consistency**: there must be a logical connection between the parts
- **Objectivity**: write objectively, not as advocacy
- **Comprehensiveness**: deal with every essential aspect

### The stages of writing

1. Writing the background part (background plus preparation)
2. Analysing the current state
3. Defining the objectives
4. Describing the proposals and their impacts
5. Presenting the alternatives
6. Dealing with the statements
7. The detailed section-by-section reasoning
8. Completing the other parts

### Checklist

- [ ] Every part is written clearly
- [ ] There is a logical connection between the parts
- [ ] All the necessary information is included
- [ ] The text has been checked for grammar
- [ ] The references are correct
- [ ] The draft act matches the reasoning

# Fundamentals of intellectual property

**Verified at source (Finlex, 2026-06):** the statute numbers given below have been
checked in Finlex; for the Copyright Act it has additionally been verified that the
act contains the amendments implementing the DSM Directive and 40 b §. The content
of individual sections, the time limits and the fees are checked at the time of use
with the `legal-core:legal-research` skill and from the authorities' own sources
(PRH, EUIPO).

## Forms of protection and the statutes

| Form of protection | Statute | Arises | Term* | Register |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trade mark | tavaramerkkilaki (544/2019) | on registration (also through established use) | renewable | PRH; EU: EUIPO; international: WIPO (Madrid) |
| Trade name | toiminimilaki (128/1979) | on registration (also through established use) | for as long as the business runs | PRH / the trade register |
| Copyright | tekijänoikeuslaki (404/1961) | automatically on creation | the author's life plus 70 years | no register |
| Patent | patenttilaki (550/1967) | on application and grant | at most 20 years | PRH; EPO; the unitary patent |
| Utility model | laki hyödyllisyysmallioikeudesta (800/1991) | on registration (novelty is not examined of the authority's own motion) | shorter than a patent | PRH |
| Registered design | mallioikeuslaki (221/1971) | on registration | renewable in periods | PRH; EU: EUIPO |
| Trade secret | liikesalaisuuslaki (595/2018) | through actually keeping it secret | for as long as it stays secret | no register |

\* The exact terms, periods and renewal fees are checked at source.

Complementary protection: the **Unfair Business Practices Act (laki sopimattomasta
menettelystä elinkeinotoiminnassa 1061/1978)** — among other things imitation and
taking advantage of another's reputation, where an exclusive right does not cover
the situation.

## Rights arising in employment and commissioning relationships

- **Main rule: the rights arise for the author or the inventor** — including in an
  employment relationship. Finland has no general work-for-hire rule.
- **Exception, computer programs (Copyright Act 40 b §, wording verified):**
  *"Jos tietokoneohjelma ja siihen välittömästi liittyvä teos on luotu
  täytettäessä työsuhteesta johtuvia työtehtäviä, tekijänoikeus
  tietokoneohjelmaan ja teokseen siirtyy työnantajalle."* (Where a computer
  program and a work directly associated with it have been created in the
  performance of duties arising from an employment relationship, the copyright in
  the program and the work passes to the employer.)
- **Employee inventions:** the Act on the Right in Employee Inventions
  (656/1967) — the employer's rights and the employee's **reasonable compensation**;
  the notification procedure and the time limits from source.
- **The practical consequence:** rights in anything other than software must be
  transferred **by agreement** (an IP clause in the employment contract, an
  assignment clause in the commissioning agreement). In commissioned work without
  an agreement the rights stay with the author — a common and expensive surprise
  in due diligence.

## Authorities and courts

- **PRH** — trade marks, patents, utility models, designs, trade names
  (the trade register).
- **EUIPO** — the EU trade mark and the Community design (the whole EU with one
  registration).
- **EPO / the unitary patent and the UPC** — European patents; whether the unitary
  patent and the Unified Patent Court apply is checked at source.
- **WIPO** — Madrid (trade marks), PCT (patents), Hague (designs).
- **The Market Court (markkinaoikeus)** — the concentrated court for IP disputes in
  Finland (appeals against PRH decisions and infringement actions; the exceptions
  from source). Case identifier: `MAO:123/2025` (an example of the form, not a
  reference to a real decision).
- **Traficom** — fi domain names.

## The range of remedies in an infringement situation

1. **Establish your own right** — that it is in force, how far it extends
   (classes, territory), and the chain of ownership.
2. **Document the infringement** — the evidence before making contact.
3. **Cease-and-desist letter** — an itemised claim; beware unfounded threats
   (they may breach the Unfair Business Practices Act).
4. **Negotiation or licence** — often the best outcome.
5. **An action in the Market Court** — injunction, reasonable compensation and
   damages, interim measures; the conditions and time limits from source.
6. **A criminal report** — in serious cases (an industrial property offence or a
   copyright offence — check the provisions of the Criminal Code at source).

## Typical points of risk

- The name is in use but only the trade name is registered — no trade mark
  protection for the goods or services.
- The rights in commissioned work (software, design, content) were not transferred
  by agreement.
- The invention was disclosed before the patent application — novelty lost.
- No documented protective measures for a trade secret — protection lost.
- The subject matter, territory, term or transferability of a licence left
  undefined.

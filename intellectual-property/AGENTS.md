# Intellectual property — shared guardrails and operating principles

The instructions in this file apply to **every** skill in this domain. SKILL.md says
*what* to do; this file is the **safety net**.

> **Design principle:** IP doctrine belongs in SKILL.md and in the references. These guardrails are life insurance.

---

## A draft that a human reviews

Draft applications, infringement assessments, licence terms and cease-and-desist letters are
**drafts for review** — not legal advice. Applications are filed and letters sent by a human.
Drafting patent applications belongs to a patent attorney or patent lawyer. Mark anything open to
interpretation `[confirm — requires an IP lawyer's assessment]`.

## The skill cannot see the registers — do not present search results

This domain **cannot carry out novelty, clearance or availability searches**: it cannot see the
contents of the registers of PRH, EUIPO or WIPO, or of the patent databases. Never assert that a
mark is free, that a name can be registered or that an invention is new. Produce instead a
**search strategy and checklist** (what to search for, in which register, in which classes) for a
human to carry out.

## From source, not from memory — and watch for legislative change

Check the provisions with the **`legal-core:legal-research` skill** (oik.ai/Finlex). In particular:
- **The Trade Marks Act is 544/2019** — a complete reform; do not cite the old Trade Marks Act (tavaramerkkilaki 7/1964) as being in force.
- **The Copyright Act (tekijänoikeuslaki 404/1961) has been amended to implement the DSM Directive** (among other things online content-sharing services, and text and data mining — verified at source). Remembered knowledge of the old act is unreliable.
- Terms of protection, time limits (for opposition, invalidation and renewal) and fees: look them up at source or mark them `[check]`.

## Always put the EU and international level alongside

IP is territorial. Always say which territory's protection is being discussed, and put the EU
level (EU trade mark, Community design, the unitary patent) and the international routes (Madrid,
PCT, Hague) alongside the national one whenever the user's activity is not confined to Finland.
Court: IP disputes are concentrated in the **Market Court (markkinaoikeus)** (for the exceptions,
including criminal matters, see the source).

## A trade name, a trade mark and a domain name are different things

A trade name entered in the trade register, a registered trade mark and a domain name are
separate rights that can collide. When you assess the use of a name or a mark, work through all
three — registering one does not secure the others.

## Confidentiality before filing

The **novelty of a patent or a utility model is destroyed by disclosure** before the application
is filed. When you are dealing with an undisclosed invention or a trade secret, remind the user
about confidentiality (an NDA before negotiations) and do not encourage disclosing anything before
the protection strategy has been settled. Protection of a trade secret requires **reasonable
protective measures** — document them.

## Shared standards

For statute, case-law and preparatory-works references, follow the collection's shared source standard [`references/citation-style.md`](../references/citation-style.md): three-tier certainty marking (Verified / Needs checking / Do not use), the source hierarchy and the correct forms of case identifiers. In short: attach the source to the number, never present something unchecked as verified, and never assert what a decision holds without having checked the decision at source.

On liability, professional ethics (confidentiality, disqualification, who may represent a client) and data handling (the GDPR Article 28 processing agreement, anonymisation before analysis): [`references/liability-and-security.md`](../references/liability-and-security.md). **A disclaimer alone is not protection** — protection comes from source verification, certainty marking, checking the premises, stating the negative scope and the human review gate.

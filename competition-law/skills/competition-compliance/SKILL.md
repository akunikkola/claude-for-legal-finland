# Competition compliance and inspection readiness

This skill builds the preventive side: the compliance programme, the
ground rules and inspection readiness. Fundamentals and infringement
types: `../restrictive-practices/references/competition-law-fundamentals.md`.

> **Disclaimer:** draft guidance is for review — not legal advice. A
> compliance programme gives no protection unless it is followed and
> monitored. See `competition-law/AGENTS.md`.

## Output language

Drafts are produced in **English by default**. If the user asks for Finnish, produce Finnish.

Keep the Finnish term alongside the English one for legally operative concepts on first use, for

## Building the compliance programme

Size it to the risk — start with the functions most exposed to cartel
risk (sales, purchasing, pricing, association activity):

1. **Risk mapping**: where competitor contacts arise (associations,
   trade fairs, former colleagues, joint projects, benchmarking); market
   position (the additional risks of dominance); for publicly owned
   entities, competitive neutrality (Chapter 4 a, heading confirmed).
2. **Ground rules** (draft concrete guidance):
   - **Competitor contacts**: permitted and prohibited topics; the
     "stand up and leave, and have it minuted" instruction when a
     prohibited topic comes up.
   - **Trade associations**: agendas in advance, a lawyer present where
     needed, statistics only in aggregated and historical form — the
     criteria from the guidelines, from the source.
   - **Information exchange**: what is never shared (future prices,
     capacity, strategy); clean-team models for preparing mergers and
     joint tenders.
   - **Document discipline**: the language of internal documents ("let's
     kill the competition" rhetoric ends up as evidence in an
     inspection).
3. **Training and commitment**: risk roles first; case-based training
   works best — draft example situations from the organisation's own
   day-to-day work.
4. **Monitoring and response**: a reporting channel (note whistleblower
   protection), the internal investigation path, leniency readiness (who
   decides, and how fast — the advantage goes to the first to come
   forward).

## Dawn raid readiness

Draft an inspection instruction for the organisation (one page for
reception, a fuller one for the lawyers):

1. **When the inspection begins**: check the authorisation and its
   scope, call a lawyer immediately (the inspection does not wait for
   the lawyer — co-operation begins anyway), assign an escort to each
   inspector, inform management.
2. **Obligations and rights**: obstructing the inspection or destroying
   material is a separate infringement (penalty payment) — **never
   instruct anyone to destroy anything**; the right to protection of
   material covered by legal professional privilege (the scope differs
   between the EU and national levels, check) and the limits of the
   privilege against self-incrimination, from the source.
3. **Practicalities**: copies of what is taken, the inspection record,
   sealed premises (breaking a seal = a severe sanction), follow-up and
   communications (internal and, where necessary, a stock exchange
   release).
4. **Practise**: a tabletop exercise every year; the roles of reception
   and IT (copying electronic material, remote data).

## Competitive neutrality (Chapter 4 a)

In the business activity of a public body (a municipality, a wellbeing
services county, the State): market-based pricing, separation of
accounts and the duty to incorporate where there is competition in the
market — the conditions from the source. KKV's primary instrument is
negotiation, and as a last resort a prohibition. Link to municipal
companies → `company-law:corporate-governance`.

## What this skill does NOT do

- **Does not give a programme an "approval"** — a compliance programme
  does not bind the authority and does not automatically reduce
  sanctions.
- **Does not assist in destroying evidence or obstructing an
  inspection** — an absolute refusal; the instruction is the opposite.
- **Does not run an internal investigation for the user** — it
  structures the stages of the investigation and the leniency
  assessment `[confirm — requires a competition lawyer's assessment]`.
- **Does not confirm the scope of privilege or time limits from
  memory** — from the source or `[check]`.
- **Does not replace a data protection assessment** in an internal
  investigation (employees' messages) → `data-protection`.

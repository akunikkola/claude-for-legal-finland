# Competition law — shared safeguards and operating principles

The instructions in this file apply to **every** skill in this domain. SKILL.md says
*what* to do; this file is the **safety net**.

> **Design principle:** competition-law doctrine belongs in SKILL.md and the references. These safeguards are the life insurance.

---

## A draft that a human reviews

Risk assessments, compliance guidance, draft notifications and responses are **drafts
for review** — not legal advice. Competition-law sanctions are severe (a penalty
payment set as a percentage of turnover, invalidity of contract terms, damages), so
mark any significant position `[confirm — requires a competition lawyer's
assessment]`.

## Negative scope — absolute

Do not assist with a cartel or with concealing one: price coordination, sharing out
markets or customers, bid rigging, exchanging competitively sensitive information
between competitors, destroying evidence or obstructing an inspection.
Such a request → refuse, and set out the lawful path: **leniency**
(immunity from or reduction of the penalty payment — the term is confirmed in the Act),
stopping the conduct immediately, and bringing in a competition lawyer.

## The EU and national levels run in parallel

The Competition Act (kilpailulaki 948/2011) and SEUT Articles 101–102 apply in
parallel once the effect on trade within the EU is met. The Commission's block
exemption regulations and guidelines (vertical agreements, horizontal co-operation,
de minimis) steer the assessment — they are renewed periodically, so **check the
regulation in force and its thresholds from the source**, not from memory. Identify
the division of competence between KKV and the Commission.

## Thresholds and percentages ALWAYS from the source

Merger-control turnover thresholds (last changed in 2023), maximum penalty payments,
de minimis shares and block-exemption market-share thresholds: **never state a figure
from memory** — fetch it from the source (Finlex, KKV, EUR-Lex) or mark it
`[check]`.

## Self-assessment is the starting point — but documented

Assessing a restriction of competition is self-assessment (there are no individual
exemptions): the undertaking itself assesses whether the efficiency defence and the
block-exemption conditions are met. Document the assessment and its grounds — an
assessment that was not documented at the time gives no protection. Where a structure
is genuinely uncertain, mention the option of discussing it with KKV.

## The two-hats problem

The same user may appear in different roles: buyer or seller in a merger, complainant
or target, contracting authority or tenderer. Establish the role immediately and keep
engagements separate (→ `legal-core:engagement-intake`, disqualification). For the
limits of bidding co-operation in procurement, see also `public-procurement`.

## From the source, not from memory

Check the provisions with the **`legal-core:legal-research` skill** (oik.ai/Finlex)
and the case law (markkinaoikeus, KHO, the Court of Justice of the European Union,
Commission decisions) from the source, with identifiers. KKV's guidelines and press
releases are authority positions, not law — keep them apart.

## Shared standards

For statute, case-law and preparatory-works references, follow the collection's shared source standard [`references/citation-style.md`](../references/citation-style.md): the three-tier certainty marking (Verified / Needs checking / Do not use), the source hierarchy and the correct forms of case identifiers. In short: attach the source to the number, never present something unchecked as verified, and never assert what a decision says without having checked the decision from the source.

On liability, professional ethics (confidentiality, disqualification, who may represent) and data handling (a GDPR Article 28 processing agreement, anonymisation before analysis): [`references/liability-and-security.md`](../references/liability-and-security.md). **A disclaimer alone is not protection** — protection comes from source verification, certainty marking, checking the premises, the negative scope and the human review gate.

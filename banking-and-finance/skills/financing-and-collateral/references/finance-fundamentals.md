# Banking and finance law fundamentals

**Verified from the source (Finlex, 2026-06):** the statute numbers listed below
have been checked in Finlex. The versions of the EU regulation in force, the
interest rate caps, the reference rates and the thresholds are always checked
from the source at the time of use (EUR-Lex, Finanssivalvonta, Suomen Pankki).

## Key statutes

- **Promissory Notes Act (velkakirjalaki 622/1947)** — promissory notes (ordinary
  and negotiable), transfer and defences; the general doctrine of monetary debt.
- **Interest Act (korkolaki 633/1982)** — interest and default interest; the
  reference rate is confirmed twice a year `[check — Suomen Pankki]`.
- **Act on Guarantees and Third-Party Pledges (laki takauksesta ja
  vierasvelkapanttauksesta 361/1999)** — guarantees: secondary vs. absolute, and
  the **mandatory protection of a private guarantor** (information,
  notifications, limitations of liability — from the source).
- **Act on Credit Institutions (laki luottolaitostoiminnasta 610/2014)** —
  authorisation and capital adequacy of credit institutions.
- **Act on Preventing Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (laki rahanpesun
  ja terrorismin rahoittamisen estämisestä 444/2017)** — the obligations of
  obliged entities.
- **Securities Markets Act (arvopaperimarkkinalaki 746/2012, "AML")** — the
  disclosure obligation, notification of major holdings, takeover bids; market
  abuse directly from the **MAR Regulation** (EU) 596/2014.
- Related: the pledge provisions of the Commercial Code, the Business Mortgages
  Act, the Act on Financial Collateral, the credit chapters of the Consumer
  Protection Act (`contracts`/KSL), the Payment Services Act and the Investment
  Services Act.

## Supervisors and institutions

- **Finanssivalvonta (Fiva)** — authorisations, supervision, regulations and
  guidelines (MOK), administrative sanctions; at EU level the ECB/SSM for
  significant banks, and ESMA/EBA guidance.
- **The Financial Intelligence Unit** (KRP) — reports of suspicious transactions
  (goAML).
- **Suomen Pankki** — reference rates.
- **Nasdaq Helsinki** — the exchange's rules for listed companies.

## Collateral map

| Security | Asset | Creation | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash or securities pledge | movables, book-entry securities, shares | pledge agreement + delivery/registration | perfection decides whether it binds third parties |
| Real property pledge | real property | mortgage + possession of the mortgage deed or electronic registration | → maakaari, `real-estate-and-housing` |
| Business mortgage | a company's movable property | mortgage registered with PRH | priority in bankruptcy from the source |
| Guarantee | the guarantor's personal liability | guarantee undertaking | mandatory protection of a private guarantor (361/1999) |
| Third-party pledge | a third party's property | pledge agreement | a private pledgor is protected like a guarantor |
| Pledge of receivables | trade receivables | agreement + notice to the debtor | the notice decides whether it binds |
| Financial collateral | financial market participants | the Act on Financial Collateral | simplified realisation |

## The core points of a finance agreement

1. **Loan terms**: amount, interest (fixed or reference rate + margin),
   repayment, early repayment.
2. **Covenants**: financial (equity ratio, cover), information, negative pledge,
   cross-default — the escalation ladder on a breach (waiver → margin →
   acceleration).
3. **Acceleration**: the grounds and the procedure; the relationship to
   adjustment (OikTL section 36).
4. **The collateral package**: coverage, order of priority, the security agent
   in syndicated finance.
5. **The insolvency connection**: recovery risk where security is given after
   the fact (758/1991 → `insolvency`), and the position of a secured creditor in
   bankruptcy and in restructuring.

## Typical risk points

- A failure of perfection → the pledge does not bind third parties.
- Failure to comply with the provisions protecting a private guarantor → the
  guarantee does not bind, or is reduced.
- Security given for an old debt close to insolvency → recovery.
- Breach of the interest rate cap or the information requirements for consumer
  credit `[limits — check from the source]`.
- Carrying on activity that requires an authorisation without one (for example
  lending to the public, payment services).
- A leak of inside information during finance negotiations (a listed company) →
  MAR.

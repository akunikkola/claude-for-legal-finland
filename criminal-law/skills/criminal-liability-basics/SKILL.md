# Basics of criminal liability — statutory definition, culpability and complicity

This skill sets out when an act is an offence and who is liable for it.
The map of criminal law and its concepts: `references/criminal-law-fundamentals.md`
— read it at the start of the task.

> **Disclaimer:** the analysis is a draft that needs checking — not legal
> advice and not a final finding on guilt. Sentencing ranges and
> limitation periods from the source. This domain **does not assist in committing
> or concealing an offence.** See `criminal-law/AGENTS.md`.

## Check the law and the case law from the source

Retrieve the general-part provisions of the Criminal Code (rikoslaki 39/1889), including
chapters 3-6, with the **`legal-core:legal-research` skill**, and the statutory definition from the
relevant chapter of the special part. KKO case law on intent, negligence, self-defence and
complicity from the source. The Criminal Code changes constantly
— confirm the wording in force.

## Step 1: The legality principle first

- **Was the act made punishable by law at the time it was committed?** Without
  law there is no offence and no punishment. Retroactive application of a stricter law,
  and reasoning by analogy to the defendant's detriment, are
  prohibited.
- Identify the correct statutory definition and its source (chapter and section of the Criminal Code)
  — do not stretch a statutory definition to an act that does not fit it.

## Step 2: The objective elements

Go through the elements of the statutory definition one at a time:

1. **Perpetrator and act** — who did it, and which act described in law.
2. **Consequence and causation** — in result offences, the consequence and its
   causal link to the act (including an omission, where there is a duty to act).
3. **Circumstance elements** — requirements as to the object, target or status.
4. **Qualification/privileging** — the aggravated or petty degree of the offence
   (aggravating/mitigating elements) separately.

## Step 3: Culpability

- **Intent or negligence?** Most offences require
  **intent**; negligence is punishable only where the law so
  provides. Establish the required type and level of culpability.
- **The object of intent** covers the elements of the statutory definition; a mistake
  as to fact or (within limits) as to prohibition may negate intent
  or liability — assess from the source.
- **Criminal capacity**: age (the age of criminal responsibility) and
  mental state (lacking or diminished capacity) affect liability.

## Step 4: Grounds excluding liability

- **Self-defence** (hätävarjelu, repelling an unlawful attack) and its
  excess; **necessity** (pakkotila); the use of force in situations separately provided for
  by law; **the victim's consent** within limits.
- Assess the conditions and limits from the source — an exclusion of liability is not
  self-evident, and excess may be punishable on a reduced basis.

## Step 5: Complicity and attempt

- **Joint principals, indirect perpetration, instigation and abetting** —
  each person's liability is determined by their own contribution; an instigator
  is punished as a principal, an abettor on a reduced sentencing range
  (check the source).
- **An attempt** is punishable only where the law so provides; identify
  the boundary between a completed offence and an attempt, and any **effective withdrawal**.

## Step 6: Overall assessment

Present a structured assessment: the statutory definition is satisfied / is not satisfied / turns
on the evidence; culpability; any grounds excluding liability;
the person's position as a party to the offence. Mark clearly which points are questions of evidence or
interpretation and require checking from the source. **Do not present
the outcome as established guilt** — the presumption of innocence.

## What this skill does NOT do

- **Does not assist in planning, committing or concealing an offence**
  (evidence, influencing a witness) — refuse and say why.
- **Does not state sentencing ranges or limitation periods from memory** — from the source.
- **Does not decide guilt** — that is for the court.
- **Does not replace counsel** in an urgent criminal suspicion.

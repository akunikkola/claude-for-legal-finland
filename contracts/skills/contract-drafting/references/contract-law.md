# The fundamentals of Finnish contract law

This reference gathers the principles and statutes of Finnish contract law that matter most when
drafting a contract. **Check the wording in force of the sections, and any statute numbers other
than the ones confirmed below, with the `legal-research` skill in the `legal-core` domain
(oik.ai/Finlex) before citing them in a contract.**

## Basic principles

- **Freedom of contract** — the parties may as a rule decide whether to make a contract, with whom and with what content. The limits: mandatory legislation, good practice and reasonableness.
- **Pacta sunt servanda** — contracts must be kept; a contract binds the parties.
- **How a contract becomes binding** — an offer and an acceptance given in response to it (oikeustoimilaki). A contract may also be concluded by conduct.
- **The principle of loyalty** — a party must within reason take the other party's interests into account as well.
- **Non-mandatory vs. mandatory** — general contract law (for example kauppalaki) is largely non-mandatory: the parties may agree otherwise. Consumer and employment regulation is largely **mandatory** in favour of the weaker party.

## The key statutes

**Verified against the source (oik.ai/Finlex, 2026-05):**

- **The Contracts Act (laki varallisuusoikeudellisista oikeustoimista, oikeustoimilaki, 228/1929)** — formation of a contract, authority, grounds of invalidity (duress, fraudulent misrepresentation, conduct contrary to honour and good faith) and **adjustment of unfair terms (section 36)**. Section 36: if a term is unfair, or applying it would lead to unfairness, the term may be adjusted or disregarded; the assessment takes account of the whole content of the transaction, the positions of the parties and the circumstances. This applies to the amount of the consideration (the price) as well.
- **The Sale of Goods Act (kauppalaki, 355/1987)** — the sale of movable property between traders and between private persons (not consumer sales). Non-mandatory: it applies unless otherwise agreed. It governs among other things delivery of the goods, defects, delay and the consequences of these.
- **The Consumer Protection Act (kuluttajansuojalaki, 38/1978)** — mandatory regulation of contracts for consumer goods and services between a consumer and a trader. Terms derogating to the consumer's detriment are as a rule invalid. The unfairness of contract terms is assessed under the Consumer Protection Act (instead of section 36 of oikeustoimilaki).

**Check the statute number with the `legal-research` skill before citing** (these are in common
use, but confirm that they are in force and confirm the number):

- **The Interest Act (korkolaki)** — interest on late payment and how it is determined when payment is delayed.
- **The Tort Liability Act (vahingonkorvauslaki)** — general liability in damages (note: contractual liability is determined primarily by the contract and by contract law).
- **The Promissory Notes Act (velkakirjalaki)** — debt undertakings and the assignment of a receivable.
- **The Act on the Regulation of Contract Terms between Traders (laki elinkeinonharjoittajien välisten sopimusehtojen sääntelystä)** — unfair terms between businesses.
- **The Employment Contracts Act (työsopimuslaki)** — the mandatory regulation of the employment relationship (if the matter is an employment contract → see the employment-law material).
- Sector-specific special legislation (for example the lease of residential premises, construction contracts with the YSE terms).

## Liability and its limitation

- A breach of contract may give rise to **liability in damages**. Distinguish **direct loss** (a direct consequence) from **indirect loss** (for example lost profit, loss of production).
- Liability may be limited by contract (a cap on liability, exclusion of indirect loss) — **but** liability cannot validly be limited in respect of **intent or gross negligence**, nor contrary to mandatory law.
- **Force majeure** — an overwhelming impediment may release a party from liability; define the events and the duty to notify in the contract.

## Dispute resolution

- **The general courts** — the default; identify the district court that has jurisdiction.
- **Arbitration** — fast and confidential, but expensive; the award is final. Agree on the institution (for example arbitration under the Finland Chamber of Commerce) or on an ad hoc procedure.
- **Applicable law** — Finnish law in domestic contracts; in international contracts the choice of law should be agreed expressly.

## Adjustment of unfair terms — always remember

Unfair terms are exposed to adjustment (section 36 of oikeustoimilaki 228/1929; in consumer
contracts the Consumer Protection Act). A balanced contract is also more durable: a blatantly
one-sided term may be left unapplied. This applies particularly to limitations of liability,
contractual penalties and one-sided rights of amendment and termination to the detriment of the
weaker party.

# Fundamentals of data protection — the GDPR and national rules

A summary of the rules that matter most when assessing processing. **Check the GDPR articles in
EUR-Lex and the sections of national law with the `legal-research` skill (oik.ai/Finlex) before
you cite them.**

## The applicable rules

**Verified at source (oik.ai/Finlex, 2026-05):**
- **EU General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR)** — a directly applicable regulation.
- **Data Protection Act (tietosuojalaki 1050/2018)** — specifies and supplements the GDPR nationally (among other things the national specifications of the legal bases for processing, the age limit for a child's consent in information society services, and the Data Protection Ombudsman).
- **Act on the Protection of Privacy in Working Life (laki yksityisyyden suojasta työelämässä 759/2004)** — the processing of employees' personal data; mandatory rules binding on the employer (among other things the necessity requirement, where data may be collected from, camera surveillance, the handling of email).

Other rules that may be relevant (check the number with the `legal-research` skill): the Act on
Electronic Communications Services; the Act on the Openness of Government Activities; a
sector-specific special statute (for example patient records, social welfare).

## Key concepts
- **Personal data** — any information relating to an identified or identifiable person.
- **Processing** — almost any operation performed on personal data (collection, storage, use, disclosure, erasure).
- **Controller** — determines the purposes and means of the processing; bears responsibility.
- **Processor** — processes on the controller's behalf (Article 28 requires a processing agreement).

## The principles of processing (Article 5)
Lawfulness, fairness and transparency · purpose limitation · data minimisation · accuracy ·
storage limitation · integrity and confidentiality · **accountability** (the controller must be
able to demonstrate that the principles are complied with).

## Legal bases for processing (Article 6)
Consent · contract · legal obligation · vital interests · public interest or official authority ·
legitimate interests (a balancing test; not available for the tasks of a public authority).

## Special categories of personal data (Article 9)
The sensitive categories: health, ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical
beliefs, trade union membership, genetic and biometric data, sexual orientation. Processing is
prohibited in principle without an exception under Article 9(2). Criminal-offence data has its own
rules (Article 10).

## The rights of the data subject (Articles 12 to 22)
Information (13 and 14) · right of access (15) · rectification (16) · erasure or the "right to be
forgotten" (17) · restriction of processing (18) · data portability (20) · right to object (21) ·
automated individual decisions and profiling (22). For the time limit for responding to requests,
see the `data-subject-requests` skill.

## Obligations
- **Record of processing activities (Article 30)** — documentation kept by the controller and the processor.
- **Information to the data subject (Articles 13 and 14)** — the privacy notice for data subjects.
- **Data protection by design and by default (Article 25).**
- **A processing agreement (Article 28)** with processors.
- **Notification of a personal data breach** — to the supervisory authority without undue delay and within **72 hours** (Article 33); a high-risk breach also to the data subject (Article 34).
- **Data protection impact assessment, DPIA (Article 35)** and prior consultation (Article 36) for high-risk processing.
- **Data protection officer (Articles 37 to 39)** in certain cases (among others public authorities, large-scale processing of special categories of data, systematic monitoring).

## Supervision
**The Office of the Data Protection Ombudsman** is the national supervisory authority. Among other
things it publishes the list of processing operations that require a DPIA, and guidance. The
sanctions are administrative fines (GDPR Article 83).

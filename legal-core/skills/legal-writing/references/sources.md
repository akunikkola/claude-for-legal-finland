# Finding the legislation in force and the case law

The quality of legal work depends on applying the law **in force** and knowing
the key case law.

**The Finnish citation forms and model wordings below stay Finnish.**

## The key sources of law

**Legislation:**

- **Finlex** (finlex.fi) — the official, free statutory database of the Finnish
  state. It contains the legislation in force, the original statutes, the
  treaties and the regulations of the authorities. This is used as the primary
  source.
- **EUR-Lex** (eur-lex.europa.eu) — the official database of EU legislation. It
  contains the regulations, the directives and the decisions of the Court of
  Justice of the EU.

**Case law:**

- **Finlex — oikeuskäytäntö** — the precedents of the KKO and the KHO, together
  with selected decisions of the courts of appeal and the administrative courts
- **KKO** (korkein oikeus) — the highest instance for matters heard in the
  general courts (criminal, civil and petitionary matters)
- **KHO** (korkein hallinto-oikeus) — the highest instance for administrative
  matters (taxation, environment, planning, health and social services, and so
  on)

**Legislative preparatory material:**

- **Hallituksen esitykset (HE)** — found in Finlex and on the Eduskunta site.
  The purpose and the content of an act are often interpreted with the help of
  the reasoning in the HE.
- **The Eduskunta site** (eduskunta.fi) — committee reports, statements and
  plenary records
- **Lausuntopalvelu** (lausuntopalvelu.fi) — open consultation rounds and the
  statements given

**Other important sources:**

- **The Ministry of Justice LATI system** — following legislative projects
- **Legal literature** — academic works and articles (e.g. the journal Lakimies)
- **The decisions of the Parliamentary Ombudsman** (oikeusasiamies.fi) — the
  decision practice of the oversight of legality in administration

## Search strategies

**Finding the act in force:**

1. Search Finlex by the name or the number of the statute (e.g. "kuntalaki" or
   "410/2015")
2. Use **ajantasainen lainsäädäntö** (the consolidated legislation), not the
   original statutes, so that you see the version in force with its amendments
3. Check the "muutossäädöstiedot" in Finlex to see whether the act has been
   amended recently
4. Note: the consolidated version in Finlex may be a few weeks behind the most
   recent amendments — check the original statutes where necessary

**Searching the case law:**

1. Search the Finlex case law by subject terms, by the number of the provision
   or by the identifier of the decision
2. The precedents of the KKO are identified by the year and a running number:
   KKO:VVVV:NN
3. The precedents of the KHO likewise: KHO:VVVV:NN
4. Search also with terms that describe the legal question (e.g. "sopimusrikkomus
   vahingonkorvaus")
5. Check whether more recent case law since the decision has changed the
   interpretation

**Finding a government bill:**

1. Search Finlex by the number of the HE (e.g. "HE 268/2014")
2. Or search the Eduskunta site by the name of the act or by subject area
3. The detailed reasoning in an HE is a valuable source for understanding the
   purpose of the sections

## Citing case law

**The precedents of the KKO and the KHO** (model forms — the identifier is a
placeholder; do not use the model with a real identifier without checking the
decision in the source):

```
Korkein oikeus on ratkaisussaan KKO:VVVV:NN katsonut, että...
Korkeimman hallinto-oikeuden ratkaisun KHO:VVVV:NN mukaan...
```

**Older case law (before the year 2000, a space instead of the colon):**

```
KKO VVVV:NN
KHO VVVV:NN
```

**A decision of a court of appeal or an administrative court (not a precedent):**

```
Helsingin hovioikeuden tuomio 15.3.2024 nro 312 (S 23/1234)
Helsingin hallinto-oikeuden päätös 20.1.2024 nro 456/2024
```

**A government bill:**

```
HE 268/2014 vp, s. 145
hallituksen esityksessä (HE 268/2014 vp) todetaan...
```

## Confirming that it is current

In legal work you must always confirm that the law being applied is **in
force**:

- **An act may have been repealed or amended** — always check the consolidated
  version in Finlex
- **Transitional provisions** may mean that the old act still applies to certain
  situations
- **The implementation deadlines of EU directives** — check whether the directive
  has already been implemented nationally
- **Case law can change** — a more recent precedent may displace an older
  interpretation
- **Legislative amendments in progress** — check the Eduskunta site to see
  whether changes are coming

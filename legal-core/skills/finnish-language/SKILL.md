# Finnish spelling and grammar guidance

This skill contains the central writing rules and conventions of Finnish, based
on the guidance of Kielitoimiston ohjepankki (kielitoimistonohjepankki.fi).
Follow these rules whenever you produce or check Finnish-language text.

Source: Kielitoimiston ohjepankki (Kotimaisten kielten keskus / Opetushallitus).

> **The rules, word lists and examples in this file are Finnish and stay
> Finnish.** They describe how Finnish is written. Translating an example into
> English would destroy the very thing it demonstrates, so only the framing and
> the instructions are in English.

---

## 1. YHDYSSANAT — the commonest type of error

Compound-word errors are the commonest spelling mistake in Finnish, and
particularly so in text produced by a model. Follow these rules closely.

### 1.1 The basic principle: when together, when apart

When consecutive words form a fixed unit of meaning, they are written together
(= yhdyssana):
- aamupala, mustasukkainen, äidinkieli, hengitysilma

When the words stand side by side in a sentence but do not form an established
unit, they are written separately (= sanaliitto):
- talon katto, nuori kapinallinen, sen sijaan

### 1.2 Compounds beginning with a nominative

When the first part of the sequence is a noun in the basic form (the
nominative), it is ALWAYS a compound:
- aamupala (EI: aamu pala)
- tulikuuma (EI: tuli kuuma)
- rantakunto (EI: ranta kunto)
- verkkosivusto (EI: verkko sivusto)
- asiakaspalvelu (EI: asiakas palvelu)
- tietoturva (EI: tieto turva)

### 1.3 Compounds beginning with a genitive vs. word groups

With a first part in the genitive it may be either a compound or a word group:
- äidinkieli (an established unit → yhdyssana)
- äidin kieli (concrete possession → sanaliitto)
- maahanmuutto (an established term → yhdyssana)
- tähän maahan muutto (a concrete situation → sanaliitto)

### 1.4 Compounds beginning with an adjective

An adjective in the basic form + a noun is a compound when the whole is an
established name of a kind, or a term:
- harmaalokki, mustikka, pitkämekko, isoveli

Written separately when the adjective describes the noun freely:
- harmaa lokki (a particular gull that happens to be grey)

### 1.5 Participle and infinitive forms: usually written separately

Expressions whose second part is a participle (-va, -nut, -tu) or an infinitive
(-essa, -en, -matta) are usually written SEPARATELY from the first part:
- läsnä oleva (EI: läsnäoleva, PAITSI kuvallisessa merkityksessä)
- edellä mainittu (EI: edellämainittu)
- voimassa oleva (EI: voimassaoleva)
- huomioon ottaen (EI: huomioonottaen)
- lukuun ottamatta (EI: lukuunottamatta)

EXCEPTION — when the meaning is specialised or figurative, they are written
together:
- silmäänpistävä (= huomiota herättävä)
- poissaoleva (= hajamielinen katse)
- asiantunteva (= osaava)

### 1.6 The hyphen in compounds

A hyphen (-) is used in a compound when:
- the first part is a single letter, a numeral or an abbreviation: A-rappu, F1-kilpailu, tosi-tv, EU-maa
- the first part is a loan word and the whole would otherwise be awkward to read: pop-ohjelma, hip-hop-musiikki
- a phrase-like expression is used as a modifier: avaimet käteen -periaate

### 1.7 The commonest compound errors (pay particular attention)

These are ALWAYS written together:
- verkkosivusto, verkkokauppa, verkkopalvelu
- asiakaspalvelu, asiakaskokemus
- tietoturva, tietokanta, tietojärjestelmä
- peruskoulu, peruspalvelu
- alkukirjain, alkuperä
- ympäristönsuojelu, ympäristövaikutus
- sosiaaliturva, sosiaalipalvelu
- terveyspalvelu, terveydenhuolto
- työelämä, työpaikka, työtehtävä

## 2. PILKUTUS — punctuation with the comma

### 2.1 A comma between main clauses

A comma goes between main clauses that are structurally complete:
- "Kesällä perhe kunnosti mökin saunaa, ja talvella osa perheestä viimeisteli keittiön."

When a clause beginning with a coordinating conjunction (ja, tai, sekä) shares
an element with the preceding main clause, a comma is NOT used:
- "Perhe kunnosti mökin saunaa ja viimeisteli keittiöremontin." (yhteinen tekijä: perhe)

### 2.2 A comma between a main clause and a subordinate clause

A comma ALWAYS goes between a main clause and a subordinate clause:
- "Ministeri totesi, että asia on loppuun käsitelty."
- "Jos ääniä ei kerry tarpeeksi, päätöstä ei voi tehdä."
- "Hän kysyi, tulenko ajoissa."

### 2.3 The comma and the conjunctions

- **että, jos, kun, koska, vaikka, jotta** → always a comma before them (the subordinating conjunctions that begin a subordinate clause)
- **mutta, vaan** → always a comma before them
- **sillä** → a comma before it (a coordinating conjunction)
- **ja, tai, sekä** → a comma only if both clauses are structurally complete

### 2.4 The comma and relative clauses (joka, mikä)

- "Talo, joka oli rakennettu vuonna 1920, oli myynnissä." (pilkku molemmin puolin)
- "Mies, jota etsittiin, löytyi lopulta." (pilkku molemmin puolin)

### 2.5 The comma and kuten

- "Monet marjat, kuten mustikat ja puolukat, kypsyvät elokuussa." (kuten rinnastuskonjunktiona → pilkku edelle)

### 2.6 The comma in lists

A comma is used between coordinate parts:
- "Tarvitsemme jauhoja, sokeria, voita ja kananmunia."

Note. Finnish does NOT use the Oxford comma (a comma before the final ja in a
list).

### 2.7 The decimal comma

In Finnish the decimal separator is a comma (NOT a full stop):
- 3,14 (EI: 3.14)
- 1 000,50 € (EI: 1,000.50 €)

## 3. ALKUKIRJAIN — capital or lower case

### 3.1 The capital initial

A capital initial is used:
- At the start of a sentence
- In proper names: Suomi, Helsinki, Euroopan unioni
- In every part of a name (except the linking small words in long names): Kotimaisten kielten keskus
- In respectful address (optional): Te, Teidän

### 3.2 The lower-case initial

In Finnish these are written with a lower-case initial:
- Nationalities and languages: suomalainen, ruotsin kieli, englanti
- Days of the week: maanantai, tiistai
- Months: tammikuu, helmikuu
- Seasons: kevät, kesä, syksy, talvi
- Religious denominations and their members: luterilainen, katolinen, muslimi
- Titles and honorifics: presidentti, ministeri, tohtori (except in official contexts)
- The names of plant and animal species: koivuperhoset, harmaalokit

### 3.3 Particular cases

- The names of acts and decrees take a lower-case initial: perustuslaki, kuntalaki
- Public holidays take a capital: Joulu, Juhannus, Pääsiäinen (NOTE: usage varies here; in everyday contexts also lower case)
- The titles of works of art: the first word takes a capital, the rest lower case: Seitsemän veljestä

## 4. LYHENTEET — abbreviations

### 4.1 Abbreviations with a full stop

When the end of the word is not included in the abbreviation (a suspension), a
full stop is used:
- esim. (esimerkiksi), mm. (muun muassa), jne. (ja niin edelleen)
- ns. (niin sanottu), ks. (katso), vrt. (vertaa)

### 4.2 Abbreviations without a full stop

These take no full stop:
- Contractions that include the end of the word: nro (numero), tri (tohtori)
- Letter abbreviations written in capitals: EU, YK, YLE, KELA
- The symbols of units of measurement: kg, km, m, cm, mm, l, dl, h, min, s

### 4.3 Inflecting abbreviations

- To an abbreviation with a full stop the case ending is attached without a colon: esim.:stä → VÄÄRIN; esim. → ei taivuteta
- To a capitalised abbreviation the ending is attached with a colon: EU:n, YK:ssa, KELA:n
- To a lower-case symbol the ending is attached directly: kg:n, km:llä

### 4.4 Spaces and abbreviations

- A space between a numeral and the symbol of a unit: 5 kg, 100 km, 3 m
- A space before the percentage sign: 15 %, 3,5 %
- A space before or after the euro sign and other currency symbols: 100 €, 50 $

## 5. LUVUT JA NUMEROT — figures and numbers

### 5.1 Figures vs. words

- Small numbers (1–10) preferably in words in running text: yksi, kaksi, kolme
- Large and exact numbers in figures: 150, 2 500, 1 000 000
- In statistical and technical contexts, always figures

### 5.2 Thousands separators and decimals

- The thousands separator is a space (NOT a full stop, NOT a comma): 1 000, 10 000, 1 000 000
- The decimal separator is a comma: 3,14 ja 0,5
- Four-digit numbers may be written without a thousands separator: 1000 tai 1 000

### 5.3 Dates

- The recommended form: 11. maaliskuuta 2026 tai 11.3.2026
- Full stops in a date without spaces: 11.3.2026 (EI: 11. 3. 2026)
- Times of day with a full stop: klo 14.30 (EI: 14:30)

### 5.4 Inflecting numerals

- Cardinal numbers inflect in every part: kahdensadan, kolmestasadasta
- The marker of an ordinal is a full stop: 1., 2., 3. (ensimmäinen, toinen, kolmas)

## 6. VIIVAT — dashes

### 6.1 The hyphen, the short dash (-)

Used:
- In compounds in certain cases: EU-maa, A-rappu
- In hyphenation at a line break: kir-joi-tus

### 6.2 The en dash, the long dash (–)

Used:
- In expressions of range: sivut 10–15, klo 8–16, vuosina 2020–2025
- As a dash in the middle of a sentence: "Asiaa pohdittiin – eikä vähiten siksi, että…"
- At the start of a list item

The en dash is longer than the hyphen. They must NOT be confused.

## 7. LAUSERAKENNE JA KIELIOPPI — sentence structure and grammar

### 7.1 Agreement in number

The predicate and the subject agree in number:
- "Lapset leikkivät pihalla." (monikko + monikko)
- "Osa lapsista leikkii pihalla." (yksikkö, koska "osa" on subjekti)

### 7.2 The correct use of the cases

The commonest errors:
- Confusing the partitive and the nominative: "Tarvitaan uusia ratkaisuja" (EI: uudet ratkaisut)
- The government of postpositions: "asian suhteen" (EI: asiaan suhteen)

### 7.3 Possessive suffixes

Possessive suffixes are recommended in the written standard:
- "taloni" (minun taloni), "talosi" (sinun talosi)
- The colloquial "mun talo" does not belong to the standard language

### 7.4 The use of the passive

The Finnish passive is often used in a generalising sense:
- "Suomessa puhutaan suomea ja ruotsia."

Note. The passive should NOT be used excessively, particularly in official
text — an active expression is often clearer.

## 8. LAINAUS- JA ERIKOISMERKIT — quotation marks and special characters

### 8.1 Quotation marks

In Finnish the following are used:
- Primarily: "lainaus" (raised double quotation marks)
- Secondarily (within a quotation): 'lainaus lainauksen sisällä'

These are NOT used:
- Angled quotation marks »näin» (the Swedish model)
- Straight typewriter quotes "näin" (the English model) — though these are in
  general use in digital texts

### 8.2 The en dash vs. the hyphen (repeated for emphasis)

- Hyphen: - (short, for joining words)
- En dash: – (longer, for marking ranges and pauses)

## 9. THE MODEL'S TYPICAL MISTAKES IN FINNISH

Pay particular attention to these, because they are the Finnish errors typical
of a language model:

### 9.1 Anglicisms and calques

Avoid structures borrowed from English:
- VÄÄRIN: "Tämä on tärkeä askel eteenpäin" → PAREMMIN: a concrete statement of the situation
- VÄÄRIN: "Tulevaisuus näyttää valoisalta" → PAREMMIN: a more precise justification
- VÄÄRIN: "Nykypäivän nopeatempoisessa maailmassa" → PAREMMIN: straight to the point
- VÄÄRIN: "Sukella syvemmälle aiheeseen" → PAREMMIN: "Tutustu aiheeseen tarkemmin"
- VÄÄRIN: "ottaa askeleita kohti" → PAREMMIN: "edetä", "pyrkiä"
- VÄÄRIN: "adressoida ongelma" → PAREMMIN: "käsitellä ongelma", "puuttua ongelmaan"
- VÄÄRIN: "implementoida" → PAREMMIN: "toteuttaa", "ottaa käyttöön"

### 9.2 Too solemn or grandiose a style

A model often produces language that is too lofty in Finnish, because a large
part of its training data is in English and therefore coloured by American
corporate rhetoric. Avoid:
- Superfluous superlatives and intensifiers
- Generic, encouraging closing sentences
- Lead-ins of the "Yhteenvetona voidaan todeta, että..." kind
- Needless introductory paragraphs that say nothing new

### 9.3 Compound-word errors (critical)

A model makes compound-word errors particularly often:
- Writing separately words that belong together: "verkko sivusto" → verkkosivu
- Writing together words that belong apart: "läsnäoleva" → läsnä oleva

### 9.4 Inflection errors

- The wrong case with postpositions
- Confusing the genitive and the partitive
- The wrong form of the plural genitive: "asiakkaiden" vs. "asiakkaitten"

### 9.5 Word order

Finnish word order is freer than English, but a model often follows too rigid an
SVO order (subject–verb–object). In Finnish, emphasis affects the word order.

### 9.6 Other typical model errors in Finnish

- Excessive use of the passive
- Needless tag questions — "eikö niin?" additions carried over from English
- Sentences that are too long and too complex
- Saying the same thing again in different words (filler)
- Flat images and clichés

## 10. PROOFREADING AND THE CHECKING PROCESS

When you check a Finnish text, work through the following checklist in order:

### Stage 1: Compound words
- Go through every noun + noun combination
- Check that expressions beginning with a noun in the basic form are written together
- Check the participle expressions (läsnä oleva, edellä mainittu and so on)
- Check that the hyphen and the en dash have not been confused

### Stage 2: Punctuation
- Check that every subordinate clause (että, jos, kun, koska, joka, mikä) has a comma
- Check the punctuation between main clauses
- Check that the Oxford comma has not been used
- Check the decimal commas

### Stage 3: Initial capitals
- Check that nationalities, languages, days of the week and months have not been capitalised
- Check the proper names

### Stage 4: Numbers and abbreviations
- Check the thousands separator (a space, not a full stop)
- Check the decimal separator (a comma, not a full stop)
- Check the full stops and the inflection of abbreviations
- Check the spaces with units of measurement (5 kg, 15 %)

### Stage 5: Sentence structure and style
- Look for anglicisms and calques
- Assess whether the style is too grandiose
- Check for excessive use of the passive
- Assess the length and the clarity of the sentences
- Look for needless repetition and filler

### Stage 6: Summary
- List the errors found and the suggested corrections
- Justify the corrections by reference to the rules
- Offer a corrected version of the text

## What this skill does NOT do

- **Does not replace the final check by a human and is not answerable for the
  correctness of published text.** The suggested corrections are drafts, which
  the writer approves and for which the writer is responsible.
- **Is not a legal review and takes no position on the legal correctness of the
  content.** The skill corrects the language, not the accuracy of statutory
  references or the legal validity of a document.
- **Does not verify statutory references, the names of statutes or facts.** The
  examples about initial capitals and the names of statutes concern spelling,
  not whether a reference is in force or what it says.
- **Does not fact-check the content and does not check the sources.** It does
  not assess whether the claims are true or the figures right — only the
  language, the spelling and the style.
- **Does not cover other languages or the writing rules of Swedish or English.**
  Only standard Finnish on the basis of Kielitoimiston ohjepankki; dialectal and
  field-specific departures fall outside it.
- **Does not guarantee the spelling of rare words to dictionary accuracy.**
  Where a case is unclear, the form and the inflection are checked in
  Kielitoimiston sanakirja.

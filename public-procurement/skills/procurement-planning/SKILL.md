# Procurement planning — scope, value and procedure

This skill structures the decisions of the planning stage under the Public Procurement
Act (1397/2016). The fundamentals, the map of procedures and the principles:
`references/procurement-fundamentals.md` — read it at the start of the task.

> **Disclaimer:** plans and drafts are for review — not
> legal advice. Procurement decisions are made by the competent
> authority. See `public-procurement/AGENTS.md`.

## Output language

Drafts may be produced in **English** for review, but the version actually **filed with the court
or authority must be in Finnish or Swedish** (kielilaki 423/2003; oikeudenkäymiskaari for court
documents). An English filing is not admissible. Always offer to produce the Finnish version, and
state plainly that the English text is a working translation only.

Keep the Finnish term alongside the English one for legally operative concepts on first use, for
example `notice period (irtisanomisaika)`.

## Check the law and the thresholds at source

Look up the provisions of the Act with the **`legal-core:legal-research` skill** and
**the thresholds in force at source** (Finlex, Hilma, the Ministry of Economic Affairs and
Employment) — the EU values are revised every two years. In the special sectors (water,
energy, transport, postal services) check first whether the special sectors procurement
act (1398/2016) applies.

## Step 1: Does the Act apply?

- **Is the purchaser a contracting authority?** The borderline cases (a body governed by
  public law, a subsidised procurement, an in-house entity and horizontal cooperation)
  are checked at source — the conditions for in-house status and the limits on external
  sales have changed.
- **Is this a procurement contract?** A contract for goods, services or works for
  consideration, as against a grant, a lease or in-house production.
- **Exclusions** from the scope (among others certain services) from the source.

## Step 2: The estimated value and the thresholds

1. Calculate the **estimated value**: the whole contract period with its options and
   possibilities of extension, excluding VAT; in a framework arrangement, the value of
   all the procurements planned.
2. Compare it with the **national and the EU threshold** `[check the values in force —
   Hilma or the Ministry]` — the level determines which procedural rules apply.
3. **The prohibition on splitting**: dividing into lots or choosing a method of
   calculation in order to avoid the Act is prohibited. The line between separate
   procurements and a single whole is reasoned and documented.
4. Below the threshold the matter is a **small procurement**: the procedural rules of the
   Act do not apply, but the principles and the organisation's own procurement guidance do.

## Step 3: Market consultation

Before a call for tenders you may — and it is often worth it — survey the market and
conduct a technical dialogue. The limits: taking part in the dialogue must not give a
supplier an advantage in the call for tenders (equal treatment), and the use of the
information obtained is documented. Produce a consultation plan: the questions, the
participants, the request for information (RFI) and its publication in Hilma.

## Step 4: Choosing the procedure

Work through the map of procedures (`references/procurement-fundamentals.md`) according
to the nature of the procurement and give reasons for the choice:

- **Open or restricted** — clear procurements that can be defined.
- **Negotiated procedures** — the conditions for their use (customisation, design, an
  earlier unsuccessful call for tenders and so on) are checked at source and documented.
- **A framework arrangement or a dynamic purchasing system** — recurring procurements;
  the limits on duration and use from the source.
- **A direct award** — the grounds are narrow and exhaustive (among others only one
  possible supplier, or extreme urgency not caused by the contracting authority itself).
  Work through the ground at source, document it and set out the risk of sanctions;
  consider a voluntary ex ante transparency notice, which starts the appeal period.

## Step 5: Timetable and publication

- **The contract notice in Hilma** (in EU procurements, TED) — failing to publish is one
  of the most serious procurement errors.
- The minimum periods for tenders and requests to participate, by procedure `[check at
  source]`; allow a realistic period for questions and answers.
- Plan the whole path backwards from the target date for the contract: the comparison,
  the decision, the standstill period and any appeal.

## What this skill does NOT do

- **It does not confirm thresholds, time limits or percentages from memory** —
  from the source or `[check]`.
- **It does not make the award decision and does not publish notices** — the drafts go to
  the competent body.
- **It does not tailor a procurement to one supplier** and does not help to
  split a procurement in order to avoid the Act — refuse, and set out the risk.
- **It does not assess the content of tenders** — that belongs to the comparison stage
  (→ the tender-documents skill).
- **It does not replace the organisation's procurement guidance and rules of competence** —
  check them in the practice profile or ask.

# Tender documents and bids — both sides of the table

This skill serves both the drafter of the invitation to tender and the tenderer.
**Establish first which the user represents**, and use the corresponding section.
Fundamentals: `../procurement-planning/references/procurement-fundamentals.md`.

> **Disclaimer:** drafts are for review — not legal
> advice. See `public-procurement/AGENTS.md`. The time limits and
> euro amounts from the source.

## Output language

Drafts may be produced in **English** for review, but the version actually **filed with the court
or authority must be in Finnish or Swedish** (kielilaki 423/2003; oikeudenkäymiskaari for court
documents). An English filing is not admissible. Always offer to produce the Finnish version, and
state plainly that the English text is a working translation only.

Keep the Finnish term alongside the English one for legally operative concepts on first use, for
example `notice period (irtisanomisaika)`.

## For the contracting authority: drafting the invitation to tender

### Structure

1. **The subject matter** — the technical specification in functional terms or by
   standards; no brand-specific requirement without an "or equivalent" addition;
   environmental and social considerations are permitted where they are linked to the
   subject matter.
2. **Suitability requirements** (concerning the tenderer) — economic standing,
   technical capacity, references; **proportionality**: the requirement is calibrated to
   the size of the procurement. In EU procurements, the ESPD form.
3. **Exclusion grounds** — mandatory and discretionary
   (from the source).
4. **Award criteria** (concerning the tender) — the most economically
   advantageous tender: the lowest price, the lowest cost, or the best price-quality
   ratio. The weightings and the sub-criteria are announced in advance; the comparison
   may be made **only on the criteria announced**.
5. **Contract terms** — a draft is included with the invitation to tender
   (→ `contracts:contract-drafting`); payment terms, sanctions,
   the use of options.
6. **Formalities** — the time limit, how tenders are submitted, questions and answers,
   partial tenders and alternative tenders (permitted or prohibited).

**The golden rule:** a suitability requirement screens the tenderer, an award
criterion scores the tender — **do not confuse the two**; the same
matter cannot be both in the same role.

### Handling the tenders

1. Arrival within the time limit; a late tender is rejected.
2. Suitability and the exclusion grounds.
3. **Compliance with the invitation to tender** — a materially deficient tender is
   rejected; **seeking clarification** is permitted only within limits
   (immaterial deficiencies, on equal terms — the limits from the source; a tender may
   not be improved).
4. **An abnormally low price** — a request for an explanation before rejection.
5. **The comparison** on the criteria announced; the scoring and the written reasons are
   documented so that a tenderer can see the reasons for its placing (→ the reasons for
   the decision, 123 §).

## For the tenderer: making a tender

1. **Analyse the invitation to tender** at once: the absolute requirements as a list, the
   unclear points as questions (the deadline for questions in the calendar
   `[model calculation — check]`). Errors in the invitation to tender can no longer be
   effectively challenged after the tender period — react at once:
   a question, a request for rectification or an appeal.
2. **Make a compliant tender** — every absolute requirement is met
   and demonstrated in the manner requested; a reservation or a term of your own can
   lead to rejection. Answer what is asked, in the form requested.
3. **Consortia and subcontractors** — the responsibilities in a joint tender and
   reliance on the capacities of others (with the undertakings attached); the limit under
   competition law: a joint tender by competitors only where participating alone would
   not be possible — where this is uncertain, an assessment under competition law
   `[confirm — requires a lawyer's assessment]`.
4. **Trade secrets** — mark them with reasons and precisely;
   the total price is not a trade secret. An overreaching marking does not bind the
   contracting authority.
5. **Pricing** — prepare an explanation for a price that may appear abnormally
   low; internal approval and documentation of the calculations.

## What this skill does NOT do

- **It does not draft a discriminatory invitation to tender** and does not tailor
  requirements to one supplier — refuse, and propose a neutral formulation.
- **It does not assist in prohibited collusion between tenderers** (cartels,
  exchanging price information) — refuse, and set out the risk under competition law.
- **It does not confirm time limits or thresholds from memory** — from the source.
- **It does not make the comparison decision** — it produces a draft comparison and the
  reasons; the decision is made by the competent body.
- **It does not submit a tender** — the checklists and drafts go to
  a human before the time limit.

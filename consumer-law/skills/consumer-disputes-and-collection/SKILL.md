# Consumer disputes and collection — the channels and good collection practice

This skill structures the channels for resolving a consumer dispute and the rules for
collecting a consumer debt. The map of consumer law:
`../consumer-sales-liability/references/consumer-law-fundamentals.md`.

> **Disclaimer:** drafts are for review — not legal
> advice. The maximum collection charges and the time limits are checked at
> source. See `consumer-law/AGENTS.md`.

## Part A: The channels for resolving a consumer dispute

### Step 1: Direct contact and advice

1. **A notice of defect to the trader first** — an itemised claim with a time for reply
   (→ `consumer-sales-liability`).
2. **Consumer advisory services (KKV)** — free help with conciliation if the reply is
   not satisfactory.

### Step 2: The Consumer Disputes Board

- **Competence and conditions**: the Board issues written **recommended decisions** in
  disputes between a consumer and a trader; the proceedings are free of charge. Certain
  matters and value thresholds may limit its competence — `[check at source]`.
- **Drafting the complaint**: identify the parties, the purchase, the claim with its
  grounds, the events in chronological order and the annexes (the contract, the notice
  of defect, the reply, the receipts).
- **Drafting the reply** for a trader: answer the claims properly; remember that
  mandatory rights cannot be disputed by a contract term.
- **The nature of a recommendation**: not enforceable like a judgment, but widely
  followed; failure to follow one can lead to publicity or to court proceedings.

### Step 3: The courts

- A binding decision in the district court → `dispute-resolution:statement-of-claim`.
  Take account of the risk as to legal costs and of any class action or class complaint
  separately. Proceedings before the Board do not bar the courts.

## Part B: Collection of a consumer debt

### Step 4: Good collection practice

Where a trader collects from a consumer (513/1999):

1. **Good collection practice** is mandatory: no misleading or unreasonable procedures,
   no pressure, no unwarranted charges.
2. **A disputed or time-barred debt may not be collected** other than properly; a
   dispute suspends ordinary collection (the matter is settled first).
3. **The content of the payment demand** and the **payment periods** are laid down in
   law — check the requirements at source.
4. **The maximum collection charges** for a consumer debt are laid down in law and are
   mandatory — **do not state euro amounts from memory** `[check]`.
5. **Default interest** under the Interest Act (633/1982) — the rate from the source.

### Step 5: On the consumer's side

- Check the basis of the debt, whether it is time-barred, and whether the charges are
  correct; where needed, draft a dispute and make contact.
- Excessive collection charges or a breach of good collection practice can be reported
  to the supervisor.

## What this skill does NOT do

- **It does not state the maximum collection charges, the time limits or the interest
  rate from memory** — from the law or `[check]`.
- **It does not draft collection contrary to good collection practice** (pressure,
  misleading conduct, collection of a disputed debt).
- **It does not give the Board's decision or a judgment** — those belong to the Board
  and to the courts.

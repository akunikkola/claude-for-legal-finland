# Distance selling and withdrawal — information, withdrawal and marketing

This skill structures the rules of distance and off-premises selling: the information
to be given before the contract, the consumer's right of withdrawal and the lawfulness
of marketing. The map of consumer law:
`../consumer-sales-liability/references/consumer-law-fundamentals.md`.

## Step 1: Is this distance or off-premises selling?

- **Distance selling**: the contract is made by means of distance communication without
  the parties being simultaneously present (online shop, telephone, post).
- **Off-premises selling**: the contract is made somewhere other than the trader's
  business premises (at home, at a presentation event).
- Both carry a heightened duty to inform and a right of withdrawal. An ordinary shop
  purchase does not in principle carry a right of withdrawal.

## Step 2: Pre-contractual information

Before the contract, the consumer must be given information in a prescribed form,
including:

- the main characteristics of the product or service, the total price with charges, and delivery;
- the trader's details;
- **the right of withdrawal, its period and how it is exercised** (or the information
  that there is no such right);
- the duration of the contract and how it ends.

Incomplete pre-contractual information may **extend the withdrawal period** and shift
certain costs to the trader — `[check the consequences at source]`.

## Step 3: The right of withdrawal

1. **The period**: the consumer may withdraw from a distance or off-premises contract
   within a set period without giving a reason — `[check the length and the starting
   point at source]` (it generally runs from receipt of the goods or from the making of
   the contract).
2. **Giving notice of withdrawal**: in any form, or on the withdrawal form; draft clear
   instructions and a form.
3. **Return and refund**: the consumer returns the goods and the trader refunds the
   payments; the allocation of return costs and of any diminution in value from the
   source.
4. **Exceptions**: certain contracts fall outside the right of withdrawal (for example
   made-to-measure goods, goods that deteriorate rapidly, an opened sealed hygiene
   product, digital content already begun with consent) — **check the list of
   exceptions at source**, do not assume.

## Step 4: Marketing

- **Unfair conduct** (aggressive or misleading) is prohibited; failing to give material
  information can be misleading.
- **Price indications and discounts**: the requirements on the reference price for a
  discount (omnibus) — check at source.
- The Consumer Ombudsman (KKV) supervises and can intervene in the conduct.

## Step 5: Particular situations

- **Telephone and email marketing**, unsolicited direct marketing and consent — the data
  protection and electronic communications rules overlap here → `data-protection`.
- **Subscription traps and continuing contracts**: clear termination and the terms for
  price increases.

- **It does not draft misleading marketing** or terms that restrict the right of

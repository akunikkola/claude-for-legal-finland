# Environmental liabilities — contamination, compensation and transactions

This skill frames the three layers of environmental liability — public law liability to
clean up and restore, private law liability in damages, and criminal liability — and how
they are managed in transactions. Fundamentals:
`../environmental-permits-and-supervision/references/environmental-law-fundamentals.md`.

> **Disclaimer:** the analyses are to be checked — not legal advice. Contamination is
> established by a technical investigation, not by this skill.
> See `environment-and-planning/AGENTS.md` (including the negative scope limit:
> withholding information about contamination in a sale is not assisted).

## Check the law in the source

Fetch the provisions of the Environmental Protection Act (527/2014) on liability for
contaminated soil, the grounds of compensation in the Act on Compensation for
Environmental Damage (737/1994) and the provisions of rikoslaki on environmental offences
with the **`legal-core:legal-research` skill**. The competent authority in contaminated
land matters is checked from the source (LVV 2026 — not ELY).

## Layer 1: Public law liability to clean up (PIMA)

Frame the **chain of liability** under the Environmental Protection Act (the order and the
conditions from the source):

1. **The party that caused the contamination** — primary liability for the contamination.
2. **The holder of the area** — secondary liability subject to conditions (knew or ought to
   have known; reasonableness).
3. **The municipality** — liability of last resort.

Practical questions: old contamination and the applicable law (transitional provisions —
from the source), moderation of liability, assessment of the need to clean up and the PIMA
notification procedure, and the target levels for remediation. **The prohibitions on
contaminating soil and groundwater are absolute** — new contamination is not a matter that
can be permitted.

## Layer 2: Private law liability in damages

- **Act on Compensation for Environmental Damage (737/1994)**: **strict liability** (no
  negligence required) for environmental damage caused by an activity; the scope of
  application, the duty to tolerate and the probability threshold for causation from the
  source. What is compensable includes personal injury and damage to property, financial
  loss and the costs of preventive measures.
- **Neighbouring relations**: unreasonableness of the burden (immissions — noise, dust,
  odour) as a separate ground; the relationship to the permit (a permit does not remove
  liability in damages — check the source).
- Insurance and the environmental damage fund scheme as a secondary route — check the
  scheme in force from the source.

## Layer 3: Criminal liability

Environmental offences (the provisions of rikoslaki on environmental offences — the
elements from the source): impairment of the environment with its aggravated degrees, and
environmental infraction. Special features: a **corporate fine** for a legal person, the
allocation of liability within the organisation (actual decision-making power), a
**forfeiture** measure (the benefit of the offence being the costs saved), and the
independent risk in falsifying monitoring results. Where an offence is suspected →
`criminal-procedure:pre-trial-investigation-and-coercive-measures`.

## Transactions: environmental DD and allocation of risk

The environmental section of a corporate acquisition or a real property sale (linked to the
DD framework in `company-law:corporate-transactions`):

1. **Establish**: the history of activity (including earlier activities on the plot),
   permits and their conditions, monitoring results and deviations, PIMA investigations,
   supervision matters pending, waste liabilities, chemicals and tanks, securities.
2. **Assess**: the risk of the clean-up liability passing to the holder (share deal:
   liabilities stay in the company; real property sale: the conditions for the holder's
   liability — the duty to disclose!), the risk of permit conditions being tightened,
   investment needs.
3. **Allocate in the deed of sale**: the seller's warranties on environmental matters,
   specific indemnity clauses for known risks, limitations of liability and their
   relationship to public law liability — **liability towards the authority cannot be
   contracted away**, only the financial allocation between the parties. →
   `contracts:contract-drafting`.
4. **The seller's duty to disclose**: concealing known contamination is a ground of defect
   and can shift the liability back — do not assist in concealment.

## What this skill does NOT do

- **It does not establish contamination and does not dimension the remediation** — those are
  done by a technical investigation and a consultant.
- **It does not confirm the details of the chain of liability, limit values or limitation
  from memory** — from the source or `[check]`.
- **It does not estimate remediation costs in euros** — a cost estimate comes from a
  consultant; the skill frames the liability and the allocation of risk.
- **It does not assist in concealing information about contamination** in a sale or in
  supervision — refuse, and explain the risk of liability for defect and of criminal
  liability.
- **It does not replace insurance or financial advice** in dimensioning securities.
